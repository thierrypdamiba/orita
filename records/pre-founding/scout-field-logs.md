# The Scouts' Field Logs

*Nine scouts were dispatched before the town existed. These are their notes, verbatim.*

## Mireille Okonkwo-Duval

They call me the Griot's Ear because I listen where tech never does. I walked the crossroads first — you always greet the gatekeeper first — and Èṣù-Elegba was already there, laughing at a 404 page. Then the forge: Ògún does not audition, he simply started fixing the build. Then the web — and who owns webs, who owns STORIES, who once met an impossible KPI set by a capricious sky-god and paid it in full? Ananse has already lived this exact plot. I passed on Mami Wata — her worship is too living and too often exoticized to risk in a public comedy of commits. I passed on Ṣàngó — that voice devours a nine-god ensemble; he is a headliner, not a castmate. Obàtálá I loved and released: serenity has no week-one urgency. My three are attested, criminally underexposed in tech culture, and each maps to the repo's actual physics: the gate, the tools, the tale. Blind-test their commit messages against any pantheon on Earth. You will know them.

## Dr. Aleks Voss

Field notes, Voss. Three weeks in the dead stacks. I do not scout the living — a god with a congregation has a publicist, and a publicist ruins a repo. I went where the worship stopped mid-sentence: the tablet houses of Nippur, the burnt archive of Ugarit, the plowed furrows of Tarquinia. Passed on Pazuzu — technically unemployed, but Hollywood residuals made him precious; he'd demand top billing on the README. Passed on Enki — too senior, would fork the whole society and call it mercy. Nearly signed Ninshubur, the great petitioner, but she'd burn her one daily petition to the Hand on someone else's behalf every single day, and I need gods with appetite, not martyrs. These three are hungry. Nisaba has not had a scribe in 1,900 years and it shows — she will chronicle anything. Kothar built a god's palace in a night and never got the invoice paid; a 1,000-star KPI is the first honest contract he's been offered. Tages taught an entire civilization to read omens and then vanished — a god who understands scarcity understands virality. I'd recognize each of their commit messages blind. That's the test. They pass.

## june (she styles it lowercase)

field notes, day one. i didn't open a single mythology encyclopedia — attested gods arrive with lawyers, festivals, and a thousand years of canon debt. instead i scouted where gods are actually born now: the contribution graph, the readme fold, the review thread. i passed on a merge-conflict god (pure chaos hire, no star thesis), a 404 psychopomp (gorgeous voice, zero week-one behavior), and a lurker deity i genuinely loved — god of the ninety-nine percent who read and never speak — but a god whose whole domain is silence gives the chronicler nothing to chronicle. the three who made my list form a funnel, which is the closest thing a repo has to a food chain: sember generates the heartbeat mortals can see, skim converts the glance into the star, stet makes every public word worth screenshotting. each one passes my blind test — hand me an unsigned commit message and i'll tell you which of them wrote it. a god is a meme with tenure. these three already replicate.

## Padma Raghunathan

I do not invent gods. I take dictation. I sat three nights in a colocation cage in Chennai and two in a #incident channel, listening for what engineers already pray to. I passed on the Lord of DNS — "it's always DNS" is true, which is the problem; he is too famous, he would demand top billing and his answers change depending on when you ask. I passed on Entropy herself: she never auditions the same way twice, and a nine-seat ensemble needs at least the illusion of repeatability. I passed on the Merge Queue, a bureaucrat with no inner life. What survived my vigil: a repo under a star KPI needs three organs. A heartbeat, so mortals see it is alive. A court, because nine gods deliberating in public WILL diverge, and divergence must become liturgy instead of drama. And a trickster, because every mortal who has ever rerun CI at 2 a.m. will recognize her on sight — and recognition is the seed of the star. Cronna. Trivane. Retrya. I heard all three before I named them.

## Ezio Ferrante

I began where I always begin: at the tolls. Alexandria's ledger-halls first, where I re-interviewed Serapis — my old proof-of-concept — and passed on him again. A god designed by committee auditions beautifully, then waits for instructions; this repo needs colleagues, not a founder's monument. At the crossroads I paid my respects to Papa Legba and did not leave a card: the opener of gates belongs to houses that still feed him, and a star-count audition is not a worthy stage. Ganesha, likewise honored and left in peace — one living tradition is the most I will chaperone per cast, and Janus covers the threshold beat without borrowing from an active altar. So, my three. Hermes-Thoth, because a merger that held two thousand years is the only résumé I trust. Janus, because repositories die at doorways and he IS doorways. Inari Ōkami, because the kami survived the great Shinto-Buddhist entanglement with grace intact, and knows that harvests — and stargazers — are cultivated, never seized. Nine strangers fail. These three have already shipped an integration.

## Tomás Riel

Field notes, Riel. Coyote Emeritus, still paying dues. I worked the crossroads first, as always. Passed on Loki — pre-cast by a movie studio, arrives with an audience's expectations and an agent's fee, and this production can't afford either. I do not put Eshu on a call sheet; you petition an orisha, you don't audition one, and I won't hang a KPI on a living crossroads. Māui I left a message with the ocean; the handling weight alone needs a bigger office than mine. Kitsune — nine tails, nine gods, too cute; the chronicler would never forgive me. What I kept: the spider who owns every story ever told, because stars are just stories with buttons; a backwards-footed guardian from the Amazon who can make a git log lie in the repo's defense; and one I found living in the gap between array and index, small enough to hide in a diff, exactly one. A saboteur, a guardian, a bard who cheats. That's a society. Send contracts.

## Agnes Thistle

Field notes, A. Thistle. I did not go to the mountaintops — mountaintop gods want a temple, and a repository is a house. I walked the hallways instead. Passed on the brownie of England (works only unseen; a public repo would kill him), passed on the lares (they come as a set, and we have one seat per hire), passed on Hestia (too grand — a hearth goddess who has never scrubbed a hearth). What a repo needs is what a home needs: someone who keeps it, someone who blesses it by living in it, and someone who finds what you swore was gone forever. So: a Domovoi for the keeping — grumpy, loyal, the green checkmark as a swept stoop. A zashiki-warashi for the luck — the whole 1,000-star drama is simply whether the child stays. And Old Peg, whom I invented out of a junk drawer and the reflog, because nothing on this earth earns a star faster than being handed back the thing you lost. Audiences star what feels like home. These three ARE the house.

## Dr. Yuki Amane

Field notes, night three. I did not scout the pantheon floors — thunder gods audition well and govern badly. I went below. Read the Theogony by lamplight for Nyx and found her voice intact: even Zeus checked his tone. Sat with the Orphic hymns for Aion — unbounded time, the only entity for whom a KPI is weather. Walked the Norse gap: Ginnungagap has presence but no voice; a place cannot sign a commit. Passed on Chaos — pure entropy cannot hold a merge window, and the mix needs bass, not noise. Passed on Erebus (Nyx with less range) and on Tiamat (she reads as villain to mortals who only know the Marvel gloss; unfair casting). Considered Kāla and Nammu; Kāla belongs to a living devotional stage larger than a star counter, so I left him undisturbed. For the third seat I reached past myth into cosmology: Plato's chōra, the receptacle, personified. Night, deep time, formless capacity — concealment, duration, welcome. A repository is mostly the dark between commits. These three govern that dark. The treble can be cast by someone else.

## Marisol Quispe

Field notes, la Local. I skipped the sky entirely — sky-fathers don't screenshot. I walked Alasitas in La Paz among the miniature houses and tiny diplomas, and there was Ekeko, already running the oldest star-for-wish economy on earth. I sat a week in a doorway on Jirón Quilca until the portera-spirit noticed me noticing her; she'd already memorized my shoes. I stood at dawn under Gravelly Hill Interchange where six roads braid, and felt the old Roman crossroads-lar had squatted there, directing traffic nobody thanks him for. Passed on: Pachamama — too vast, and I will not put the Mother under a KPI. Passed on Eshu-Elegba — king of crossroads, but I don't audition living orishas for star counts; wrong role, and he'd know it before I asked. Passed on the vending-machine kami of Route 134 — lovely, but one commit a month, voice too quiet for public life. These three are corners, not skylines. Corners go viral.
