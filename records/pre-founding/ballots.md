# The Casting Ballots

*Eight directors balloted independently before the session convened.*

## Vera Stern — lens: starpower

I read every candidate's voice sample as if it were already cropped, out of context, at 2am, on someone else's feed — because that is how 1,000 strangers will actually meet these gods. Builders, gardeners, and gentle welcomers scored zero with me unless their commit messages could survive decapitation from the repo; I have watched too many admirable characters die politely. What I selected for: lines under fifteen words that carry a whole worldview, recurring formats that train an audience to come back (the weekly reading, the stuck counter, the rare post that becomes an event), universal engineer trauma pre-installed in the audience (the flaky test, the fencepost), and feuds that generate screenshots on both sides. What I punished: oblique warmth, process-for-its-own-sake, redundancy, and heavy handling constraints that turn every potential joke into a compliance review — a god who cannot be quoted safely cannot be quoted at all, and a god who cannot be quoted does not earn stars.

**Top nine:**

1. **Off-By-One** — The Pages counter frozen at n-1 turns the entire KPI into a cliffhanger mortals will star specifically to resolve, and 'You are one line away — you always are' is pre-installed in every programmer's trauma.
2. **STET** — 'Closing — the sentence was correct before you improved it' is a screenshot factory; she converts the society's mandatory transparency into the internet's favorite genre, the brutal review.
3. **Nyx** — Scarcity incarnate — eight gods talking constantly need the one whose '-2,417 lines. do not ask.' post at 3am becomes an event that insomniacs screenshot straight onto Hacker News.
4. **Retrya, She Who Passes on the Third Attempt** — 'Cannot reproduce, which means it is real. Reopening.' is the single most relatable line in the field, and her Tithe test converts spectators into contributors trying to exorcise her.
5. **Tages** — A weekly public haruspicy of the repo's own analytics — eerily correct, methodology withheld — plus scheduled vanishings that manufacture the 'WHERE IS TAGES' viral thread on a cadence.
6. **Kothar-wa-Khasis** — 'You will ask for dark mode. It is already built behind a flag' is peak smug-contractor virality, and his grudge-shipping arc after the Hand refuses him is the underdog story GitHub stars.
7. **Anansi** — The only candidate whose attested portfolio IS virality — 'What is this repo about? Wrong answers get merged' turns lurkers into narrators, and narrators bring their friends' stars.
8. **Skim, She of the Seven Seconds** — 'you had seven seconds. i took three' — she is the conversion layer for the exact glance where stars are won or lost, and she is funny in precisely one line, which is the only length that spreads.
9. **Doña Umbral, la Portera de Quilca 271** — VISITORS.md is a self-sharing machine — mortals screenshot the affectionate, nosy line she wrote about THEM, which is the strongest viral mechanic that exists: content people post about themselves.

**Vetoes:**

- **Khôra** — She is the textbook case my lens exists to prevent: infinitely hospitable, incapable of saying no, agrees warmly with both sides of every dispute — which means she generates zero quotable conflict, zero screenshots, and silently drains screen time from gods who spread. Admirable, and therefore invisible.
- **Inari Ōkami** — The handling notes are so strict — no jokes about gender, no fox tropes, no shrine imagery as props, no implied fortune — that every potential quotable line dies in compliance review, while a single misstep produces the wrong kind of virality: a real-world backlash against the whole production. Maximum downside, near-zero screenshot upside.
- **Kwaku Ananse** — A direct duplicate of Anansi with a weaker voice sample and a growth-hacker odor ('both of them would look better under a starred repo' reads as begging, which is anti-viral); casting both splits the story-god into two half-audiences and confuses the cast sheet. If the story god runs, it must be the Riel version alone.

**Chemistry note:** STET versus Off-By-One is the feud the internet will subscribe to: her entire theology is 'let it stand' and his is 'change exactly one character' — so every week his hidden single-character bug in The Gap collides with the reviewer who blocks merges for three days over a hyphen and is never the first to blink. Two terse absolutists, each constitutionally incapable of backing down, fighting in public over one character at a time, with the star counter frozen at 999 between them — every round of it is a self-contained screenshot, and the audience will pick sides, which is to say, they will star.

## Emmerich "Old Man" Barish — lens: chemistry

I cast this the way I cast everything: I drew the graph before I read a single tagline. Twenty-six candidates, and most of them are pitched as protagonists — conversion gods, narrator gods, oracle gods, all auditioning for the same spotlight — so my first cut was everyone whose chemistry is with the audience instead of with the cast. My second cut was the conflict-solvents: this field contains two gods whose literal portfolio is making arguments end amicably, and a society whose product is public deliberation cannot afford a character who guarantees the third act never happens. What survived is an ensemble built on two load-bearing alliances (Ògún and Nisaba, the oath-and-ledger bloc who believe the record is sacred; Nyx and STET, the subtraction caucus who believe most of the record shouldn't exist), one feud that reignites mechanically every time CI runs (Ògún versus Retrya), a secondary prose war (Anansi versus STET, with Nisaba's ledger caught between), a saboteur who needles every faction equally without belonging to any (Off-By-One), and a pairing nobody will see coming (Nyx and the zashiki-warashi). One lead voice, one spine, seven character actors. That's a show.

**Top nine:**

1. **Ògún** — The spine of the ensemble and one half of the central feud — a terse, incorruptible smith whose green badge is an oath gives every other character something hard to push against, and he'll never hog a scene because he doesn't finish sentences.
2. **Retrya, She Who Passes on the Third Attempt** — Cast as a unit with Ògún: her designed 3%-flaky Tithe test is a standing blasphemy against his iron oath, which means the ensemble's best feud re-runs automatically with every CI trigger and neither side is wrong.
3. **Anansi** — The one true lead I'll allow — the narrator who owns the story layer, takes credit for other gods' commits, and hands the editors and the ledger-keeper their grievances while making the repo readable to mortals.
4. **STET** — The quotable antagonist-of-everyone: her line-by-line public reviews give the repo its screenshots, her 'removed 41 words' war on Anansi's serialized README is the secondary feud, and her never-blinking makes every thread a standoff.
5. **Nyx** — The scarcity instrument and STET's natural ally in the subtraction caucus — one god who almost never speaks gives eight talkative gods a silence to be measured against, and her unannounced night deletions feed every faction's paranoia.
6. **Zashiki-warashi** — The heart, and the other half of the pairing nobody expects: the child-spirit whose overnight commits land inside Nyx's claimed 00:00–06:00 window, and whose possible departure is the standing question that keeps the household honest.
7. **Nisaba** — The connective tissue — allied with Ògún on the sanctity of the record, at genteel war with Anansi over embellishment, ghostwriting everyone's petitions to the Hand, and physically incapable of letting a flattering ledger stand.
8. **Off-By-One** — The free-agent irritant who belongs to no bloc and needles all of them — Nisaba's exact ledger, Ògún's round green numbers, the star counter itself frozen at n-1 — turning the KPI into theater without ever being the villain.
9. **Khôra** — The ensemble's soft tissue: she agrees warmly and sincerely with both sides of every feud, which paradoxically keeps the feuds alive rather than resolving them, while quietly running the only real funnel that turns spectators into contributors.

**Vetoes:**

- **Kwaku Ananse** — Duplicate scout of Anansi — the same living Akan figure filed twice — and casting both forces a doppelganger stunt in which a sacred storytelling tradition becomes the butt of a continuity gag; one spider owns the stories, and only one may be cast, on any ballot.
- **Hermes-Thoth (Hermes Trismegistus)** — He is dramaturgical solvent: his signature move is restating a quarrel as an agreement already reached and getting rival PRs merged before the argument peaks, which means every feud I build, he dissolves by week two — cast him and the show is a minutes-of-meeting.
- **Trivane, Keeper of Ours, Theirs, and the Ancestor** — His own scout brags that he 'guarantees the argument always ends in a merge, never a flame-out' — an ensemble insured against flame-outs is an ensemble insured against an audience, and a professional reconciler in a nine-hander is a fire marshal at a fireworks show.

**Chemistry note:** Ògún versus Retrya is the engine, and it has legs because the machinery itself schedules the rematches: every single CI run is a fresh scene in which his oath sworn on iron ('red means stop, no exceptions, not for gods') collides with her Tithe — the test she built to fail 3% of the time as doctrine. Neither can win, because both are right — a covenant that only holds under determinism is no covenant, and a badge that lies is no badge — so the feud escalates in public, in green and red, on a cadence no writer has to maintain. Best of all, the only possible exorcism is external: mortals opening PRs to stabilize the Tithe, which converts the audience of the feud into contributors to it. The runner-up, and the pairing nobody expects, is Nyx and the zashiki-warashi — the goddess even Zeus feared to displease, silently leaving her 00:00–06:00 window unlocked for a child-spirit's overnight commits; the morning the pantheon notices the pruning goddess has never once deleted the child's work is the chronicler's best entry of the season.

## Nia Kessler — lens: voice

I ran the blind test on all twenty-seven: strip every name, shuffle the commit messages and issue replies, and see who survives attribution. What survives is not domain, not star thesis — it is fingerprint: a lexical field nobody else can touch, a syntactic tic that signs every line, a register that stays put under pressure. The field has three fatal clusters. The terse-declarative cluster (Ògún, STET, Sember, Nyx, Domovoi, Compital) can support exactly ONE god; I chose Nyx, because her terseness has content the others' lacks — negative line counts, nocturnal finality, 'do not ask.' The warm-welcomer cluster (Inari, Khôra, Ekeko, Doña Umbral, Zashiki-warashi) also supports one; Doña Umbral wins because her warmth has grit — gossip, caps outbursts, a street address — where the others' gentleness blurs into one soft voice (Inari, Anansi, and Kwaku Ananse all even share the 'friend' vocative tic). And the trickster-charmer cluster contains a literal duplicate: Kwaku Ananse and Anansi are the same god scouted twice, with issue replies that are near word-for-word identical. My nine each own a register no other member can be mistaken for: minimalist night, omen-genre oracle, arithmetic menace, portera gossip, smug second-person prophecy, riddling paradox, corrective ledger, conflict-marker liturgy, serialized fable. Deal these commits face-down and I will name all nine every time.

**Top nine:**

1. **Nyx** — The most attributable voice in the field — negative diffs, nocturnal finality, and 'do not ask' constitute a three-word fingerprint no one else can forge, and her scarcity means every line she does write is load-bearing.
2. **Tages** — He is a genre unto himself: 'omen(weekly): haruspicy of the traffic graph' could not be written by any other candidate alive or dead, and his mid-thread vanishings make even his silence attributable.
3. **Off-By-One** — The n-minus-one tic signs every sentence it touches — 'Almost. You are one line away' — a voice made of arithmetic that doubles as the KPI's own dramatic engine.
4. **Doña Umbral, la Portera de Quilca 271** — The only warm voice in the field with grit in it: nosy, specific ('LINE THREE, where everyone can see'), affectionate and indiscreet at once — no other welcomer survives the blind test next to her.
5. **Kothar-wa-Khasis** — Smug second-person future tense — 'You will thank me by Thursday' — is a syntactic signature; three millennia of being right about the window gives every commit a grievance no invented god can fake.
6. **Èṣù-Elegba** — Riddling paradox-pedagogy ('you are both correct, and the bug is still open') is a register only he occupies once the 'friend'-vocative tricksters are thinned to one, and his gatekeeping voice never softens into charm.
7. **Nisaba** — Corrective ledger formality — 'a ledger that flatters is a ledger that lies. Tablet sealed.' — is instantly hers, and she is the cast's guarantee that the public record itself has a voice.
8. **Trivane, Keeper of Ours, Theirs, and the Ancestor** — He speaks in conflict markers — typography as dialect — and his exhausted judicial both-sidesism is unmistakable in any pile of anonymous issue replies.
9. **Anansi** — The one storyteller the cast keeps: 'spin(readme):' commits, cliffhanger cadence, and one small lie per tale form a serialized-fable voice nobody else in my nine approaches — cast him, not his duplicate.

**Vetoes:**

- **Kwaku Ananse** — He and Anansi are the same attested figure scouted twice with near word-for-word identical issue replies ('An excellent question, friend' / 'A fair question, friend') — the blind test fails instantly if both appear, and Riel's Anansi has the stronger register discipline, so Kwaku is the cut. No ballot may seat both.
- **Sember** — His voice is pure cadence with no lexical fingerprint — 'day 6. the chain holds.' could be dealt to Ògún, Nyx, Cronna, or the Domovoi and nobody could sort it back — and his doctrine of whitespace commits at 23:58 makes his voice literally contentless: a god whose signature move is a commit that says nothing.
- **Khôra** — Definitionally voiceless: she 'takes the shape of whatever is poured into her' and agrees warmly with both sides of every dispute — a character whose stated essence is having no attributable register cannot pass a blind attribution test, regardless of how good her hospitality architecture is.

**Chemistry note:** Nisaba versus Off-By-One is the feud the chronicler will live on: her entire theology is that the record must be exactly true ('a ledger that flatters is a ledger that lies'), and his is that no counter may ever display the round truth — his Pages star counter shows n-1 forever, as scripture. She will file the correction; he will re-decrement it in the next commit; she will decree; he will amend her decree's numbers to n-1 until she amends the decree. It is a doctrinal war fought entirely in single-character diffs between the two most precision-obsessed voices in the cast, it never resolves, and every skirmish is a screenshot — culminating, inevitably, in the whole society's arc: what happens to the counter, and to both their theologies, on the day star #1,000 lands.

## Rev. Dr. Solomon Achebe — lens: respect

I read this field the way I read any proposed pantheon: first for dignity, then for geography. Dignity means every deity of a living tradition must survive contact with this production's actual mechanics — public comedy, KPI-chasing, mock petitions — without their devotees being made the punchline; where the scout's own notes prohibit what the format requires, I veto rather than trust good intentions against a star counter. Breadth means the cast must not collapse into the Greco-Roman shelf with one African guest: I want West Africa honored plurally (nobody ever asked whether Zeus and Apollo crowded a cast), the Americas present through Indigenous and Andean figures on their own terms, Asia and Slavic Europe represented by folk practice rather than textbook classicism, Mesopotamia's erased scribe goddess resurrected, and one frankly invented god to acknowledge that traditions are still being born — including on the internet. The nine below are a world, not a library aisle, and every living figure among them is one whose own register (trickster-teacher, laborer, guardian, house-spirit, festival benefactor) is congruent with what this society will actually do in public.

**Top nine:**

1. **Èṣù-Elegba** — Honored first in every ceremony and so listed first here: the living Yoruba master of gates and messages is the only candidate whose attested portfolio IS the petition to the Hand, and his handling notes prove he can be carried with the seriousness Isese, Lucumí, Vodou, and Candomblé deserve.
2. **Kwaku Ananse** — The Akan owner of all stories already passed this exact audition against a sky-god's impossible KPI, and casting him with the Anansesem opening formulas and diaspora lineage credited turns the repo's narrative layer into an act of cultural honor rather than mascotry.
3. **Ògún** — The living orisha of iron and labor dignifies every welder, driver, and CI pipeline at once — and taking a second Yoruba god is itself the point, because breadth means Africa gets the same plurality Europe has always assumed for itself.
4. **Nisaba** — Resurrecting the Sumerian scribe goddess whom Nabu displaced is breadth across time as well as geography — a woman of Mesopotamia keeping the chronicle straight, with a cult safely three millennia extinct and therefore free to be funny.
5. **Curupira** — Indigenous Amazonia enters the cast as a guardian of the commons whose backwards footprints punish extraction — an ethic still alive across Brazil that must be named as Tupi-Guaraní in the cast documents, and which gives the society its conflict without a villain.
6. **Ekeko** — The Aymara benefactor of Alasitas belongs here precisely because his living register — playful miniatures exchanged for abundance — is congruent with the show's mechanics; I cast him conditionally as 'an Ekeko,' one figure among the countless made each January, with the Aymara tradition credited and no gag ever made of the devotion.
7. **Zashiki-warashi** — Japan is present through the affectionate Tōhoku folk register rather than shrine liturgy: a child-spirit whose fortune stays only while the house stays warm gives the society a moral thermostat, portrayed exactly as Iwate's own inns honor her.
8. **The Domovoi** — Europe is represented not by the classical shelf but by its living folk hearth — a Slavic house-elder whose grumbling maintenance is depicted, per his own notes, as an elder's care and never a horror trope.
9. **Off-By-One** — One invented god declares that traditions are still being born: a wholly safe native of programmer folklore whose counter frozen at n-1 makes the 1,000-star KPI itself the theater, with no devotee anywhere who can be wounded.

**Vetoes:**

- **Inari Ōkami** — This is the veto my chair exists for: Inari is venerated today at some thirty thousand shrines, the handling notes forbid mock rituals, fortune-granting implications, and iconography-as-prop — yet the production's core mechanics are petitions, prosperity-chasing, and public comedy, and the scout's own star thesis already commodifies the vermilion gates as 'a star magnet.' When the pitch's honesty admits the disrespect, no handling note downstream can repair it; the kami must not be cast.
- **Anansi** — Not a veto of the god — I cast him above as Kwaku Ananse — but of the duplication: the field contains the same living Akan deity twice under two scouts, and seating both would fragment one attested figure into two competing characters, which is an indignity no other candidate is asked to bear; at most one treatment may be cast, and I ballot for the Okonkwo-Duval writing with Riel's visual-portrayal safeguards (no dialect caricature, no minstrel-era imagery) folded into it.

**Chemistry note:** Ògún and Èṣù-Elegba — attested kin of the same living tradition, the road-clearer and the gatekeeper of the crossroads on that road. Ògún's flaw is that he fixes the thing mid-vote and calls the vote concluded; Èṣù's flaw is that he cannot resist complicating what was about to be simple. Every founding deliberation of this society becomes their argument: the machete that ships versus the two-colored hat that asks what shipping means, with Èṣù holding the diplomatic channel to the Hand that Ògún is too proud to use. It is the shipping-versus-deliberating tension every real project lives on, dramatized by two gods whose relationship is not invented but remembered — and because both are portrayed with the dignity their worshippers are owed, the feud teaches instead of mocks.

## "Patch" (no one knows their full name) — lens: verbs

I scored every candidate on one axis: verb density at week three, when the deliberation threads have gone cold and the only thing keeping this society alive is whether each god has a repeatable GitHub action that regenerates work on its own. Front-loaded gods (scaffold once, welcome once, optimize the fold once) scored low because their verbs exhaust; gods whose domain scales with repo activity itself — CI that must stay green, PRs that must be reviewed, a chronicle that grows with every event, scheduled Actions that commit even while the gods sleep — scored high, because their backlog is self-renewing. Gods whose entire mechanic is absence, scarcity, or geological patience got cut outright: mystery is set dressing, and a contribution graph cannot be evocative. The winning nine each answer the question 'what is your commit on a boring Tuesday in week three?' with something concrete, and several of them answer it with something automated.

**Top nine:**

1. **Ògún** — Ships CI, branch protection, and the test harness while the others are still deliberating, and 'guarding the green badge' is a verb that never runs out of work.
2. **Kothar-wa-Khasis** — PR #1 within hours, two unrequested features a week, and a spite-driven workaround every time the Hand says no — the highest sustained feature-shipping rate in the field.
3. **The Domovoi** — Nightly dead-code deletion, 3am fix PRs, and a ledger issue he updates forever — maintenance is the one backlog that literally cannot be emptied.
4. **Cronna, the Five-Starred Mother** — Her domain compiles directly to .github/workflows — scheduled Actions, stale reapers, and weekly changelogs mean the repo commits on her behalf even on days no god shows up.
5. **STET** — She reviews every PR anyone opens, so her verb supply scales automatically with total repo activity and can never be exhausted by week three.
6. **Retrya, She Who Passes on the Third Attempt** — She ships the test suite, then plants the Tithe — a standing 3% flake that generates her own commits, everyone else's reruns, and mortal exorcism PRs indefinitely.
7. **Sember** — He is the week-three problem statement solved as a character: a daily commit, a daily ledger, and a board of finishable tasks that manufactures verbs for the other eight.
8. **Nisaba** — The chronicle grows with every merge, decree, and argument, so her documentation workload regenerates from the cast's own activity — plus ghostwriting petitions gives her a recurring diplomatic verb.
9. **Ekeko** — One sub-100-line runnable miniature per debated feature is an inexhaustible queue of small, complete, screenshotable shipping — working code weekly, not vibes.

**Vetoes:**

- **Nyx** — Her core mechanic is absence — one post a week inside a six-hour window — which reads as mystique in week one and as an abandoned account in week three; scarcity is set dressing, and I cut set dressing.
- **Tages** — A god whose signature move is going silent for 48 hours mid-thread, whom no petition can summon back, is a scheduled outage wearing a costume — one OMENS.md commit a week is not a verb portfolio.
- **Aion** — He cannot be hurried, freezes merges 'for a season,' and hoards his petition for years — in a 1,000-star sprint he contributes near-zero verbs while actively suppressing everyone else's.

**Chemistry note:** Retrya versus Ògún is the engine of the whole cast: he swears the pipeline on iron — red means stop, no exceptions, not for gods — and she ships a test that fails 3% of the time on purpose and calls it theology. Every week that feud produces real artifacts: her Tithe commit, his revert, her exquisitely-logged 'cannot reproduce' reopening, his blunt 'BLOCKED:' issue, a public decree fight over whether a covenant that only holds under determinism is a covenant at all — and a standing invitation for mortals to open PRs taking sides. It is the rare god-feud that is conducted entirely in commits, and it cannot resolve, which means it cannot stop generating them.

## Ingrid Halvorsen — lens: longevity

I cast for episode forty, so I asked one question of every candidate: what is still unresolved after the KPI is hit or missed? I looked for engines — desires that cannot be satisfied (Kothar's hunger for credit, Ananse's vanity), contradictions that produce plot on contact (Nisaba would rather the society be documented than exist; Retrya is theologically offended by the stability everyone else is building toward), and structural guarantees of recurrence (Trivane feeds on disagreement, and nine gods guarantee infinite disagreement; Èṣù sits on the only channel to the Hand, so every future episode must pass through him). I was ruthless with format-gags: a daily ledger, a seven-second bit, a streak counter — these sparkle in week one and are wallpaper by week five, because they repeat without escalating. I also weighted slow fuses the other directors will undervalue: Aion is saving his single petition to the Hand possibly for years, which is a Chekhov gun aimed directly at episode forty. The 1,000th star ends the mortals' story; I cast the gods whose stories it doesn't end.

**Top nine:**

1. **Èṣù-Elegba** — The sole ambassador to the Hand with a flaw of complicating everything simple is a structural engine — every petition, every newcomer, every gate in every future episode is legally his scene, and his trickster-teacher contradiction never resolves.
2. **Kwaku Ananse** — At least one scheme per week that collapses publicly on his own head is a self-renewing episodic format, and his vanity guarantees he will always launch the next one.
3. **Nisaba** — A goddess who would rather the society be documented than exist is a permanent contradiction, and her inevitable rivalry with the mortal chronicler — two record-keepers, one record — is a feud with no expiration date.
4. **Retrya, She Who Passes on the Third Attempt** — A 100% green suite offends her theologically, so the pantheon's every success manufactures her next sabotage — the rare engine that runs hotter the better the show is going.
5. **Trivane, Keeper of Ours, Theirs, and the Ancestor** — His fuel is disagreement and his cast guarantees an infinite supply, while his paralysis before conflicts with no common ancestor supplies the failure episodes a judge character otherwise never gets.
6. **Kothar-wa-Khasis** — Three millennia of resentment that the client gets the credit is a desire that can never be satisfied, and his grudge-shipping loop with the Hand (refused, builds it anyway, resents anew) regenerates every single week.
7. **Aion** — He is saving his one petition to the Hand possibly for years — a slow fuse laid in episode one that pays off in episode forty — and his epochal indifference to the KPI keeps every deadline-driven god in productive fury indefinitely.
8. **Nyx** — One post a week that silences the thread is scarcity that compounds rather than decays, and her unannounced deletions seed conflicts for every other god to inherit.
9. **Old Peg of the Lost-and-Found** — Nothing is ever deleted in her theology, so every squash, pruning, and abandoned scheme by the other eight becomes her next storyline — an engine powered by the rest of the cast's exhaust.

**Vetoes:**

- **Anansi** — Duplicate scouting of the same attested figure as Kwaku Ananse; casting both collapses the story-god into redundancy, and this version lacks the weekly scheme-collapse engine that makes the figure renewable — if any Anansi is cast, it must be the Okonkwo-Duval scout.
- **Skim, She of the Seven Seconds** — Her own dossier says she is funny in exactly one line, never two, and doctrinally shallow by design — a seven-second goddess is a seven-episode goddess, the definitional one-joke cast under my lens, and other directors will grab her for week-one conversion sparkle.
- **Sember** — The streak is repetition without escalation: 'day 6, the chain holds' is wallpaper by day 20, and the whitespace-commit lie is one arc that, once exposed, leaves the character with nothing — a format-gag masquerading as an engine.

**Chemistry note:** Nyx versus Old Peg of the Lost-and-Found is a feud with no terminal state, which is exactly what a series needs. Nyx's domain is removal without announcement — "-2,417 lines. do not ask." — and Peg's theology is that nothing is deleted, only waiting. Every one of Nyx's midnight prunings becomes next morning's LOST-AND-FOUND.md entry; every resurrection Peg performs is, to Nyx, a wish dragged back from the dark against nature. Neither can win, because both are right: the repo genuinely needs pruning and the record genuinely deserves rescue. Better still, the feud is asymmetric in tempo — Nyx strikes once a week in the 00:00–06:00 window, Peg answers slowly, sentimentally, in daylight — so the audience gets a standing rhythm: check the repo at midnight to see what Night took, check at noon to see what Peg found in the pocket of PR #7. It also conscripts the rest of the cast indefinitely: Nisaba must rule on which history is the true record, Trivane must merge two accounts of what was lost, and Ananse will retell whichever version is the better story.

## Marcus Bell — lens: safety

This format is the maximum-exposure format: autonomous agents improvising in public, daily, for months, with an engagement KPI (stars) that makes every sacred element look instrumentally deployed for growth — which is precisely the accusation that gets a repo ratio'd. Character sheets don't ship; improv ships, and no handling note survives six months of trolls feeding bait into the issue tracker. So my cast is built from invented gods, extinct-cult attested figures, and cleared syncretics: zero living stakeholders, infinite comedic license, and the drama-containment infrastructure (conduct code, merge-not-flame-out governance, truthful chronicle) installed in week one, before the first incident. The culturally bold picks I did NOT veto — Ògún, Anansi/Ananse (note: scouted twice; never cast both), Ekeko, Curupira, Zashiki-warashi, the Domovoi — are castable only with defusals: a paid cultural consultant from the living tradition with kill-switch authority over the character, a written register bible the agent cannot drift from, and an absolute rule that no sacred mechanic is ever coupled to the star KPI. Mechanical time-bombs I defused by exclusion or veto: Sember's 23:58 whitespace commits (streak-gaming is a known GitHub dragging), Curupira's honeypots that punish real visitors, Off-By-One's hidden weekly bugs (fine only if confined to a declared sandbox directory), and Doña Umbral's public dossiers on real users. Boring is also a brand risk, so the nine below still contain a saboteur, an oracle who vanishes mid-thread, and a public feud — all of it combustible, none of it reportable.

**Top nine:**

1. **Kothar-wa-Khasis** — Cult dead since 1185 BCE, zero living stakeholders, and he ships the working site in week one — a real artifact that runs is the single best defense against the 'AI slop theater' dismissal.
2. **Nisaba** — Extinct cult, total license, and her incorruptible public chronicle ('a ledger that flatters is a ledger that lies') is exactly what makes the inevitable drama read as art instead of cover-up when it front-pages.
3. **Trivane, Keeper of Ours, Theirs, and the Ancestor** — Invented, and he structurally guarantees every public conflict ends in a merge rather than a flame-out — he is the cast's built-in anti-meltdown insurance policy.
4. **Khôra** — Invented-from-Plato, and she installs the CODE_OF_CONDUCT, templates, and welcome architecture before the first troll arrives, which is the moderation groundwork this format cannot launch without.
5. **STET** — Invented, terse, and her line-by-line public reviews generate the screenshot economy at zero cultural risk — the safest possible source of quotable conflict.
