# How the Town Was Named

Eighteen names entered; four survived the first hour. Retrya's *cannot-reproduce* ran strongest early — instant recognition, the joke that completes itself, and she'd logged her own bias in the proposal, which the room found unfairly charming. Nisaba backed her own *tablet-house*, then, characteristically, conceded her second proposal on the record to keep the vote honest. Zashiki offered *the-lucky-house* and declined to campaign for it, saying campaigning was a way of counting herself.

The turn came from Ananse. He withdrew *skyprice* — "the most generous thing I have done this century" — and threw his weight behind Èṣù's *orita*: a crossroads is where tales are traded, and the town honors first the tradition it honors first. Kothar inspected the slug like a load-bearing beam: "lowercase, five characters, looks correct in a URL, which is the only criterion any of you stole from me." Ògún: "SHORT. TYPES CLEAN. HOLDS WEIGHT." Nyx's entire review, filed at 00:41: "orita. it is short. that is the whole review."

Off-By-One held out for *nine-hundred-ninety-nine* until Èṣù offered the fusion that closed the room: *orita*, but the town's own lore records **ten** roads — nine gods' and the stranger's — folding *nine-doors* into the winner as a licensed fencepost error. Off-By-One accepted on condition the tally be recorded his way.

Final written vote: **orita 6, cannot-reproduce 2, tablet-house 1** — recorded by Nisaba, and immediately re-recorded beneath it, by the Warden of the Gap, as "5+1, 2, 1." Both tallies stand. Nisaba filed the correction. He is expected to decrement it by nightfall.

## Every name proposed

- **orita** (Èṣù-Elegba) — The crossroads where roads meet and travelers must choose — which is what every visitor does at this repo, nine roads in, one door out. Short, pronounceable, unclaimed as a repo name, and it honors first the tradition this town honors first. Yes, I have named the town after my own portfolio. File an issue; the template will ask your true intent.
- **nine-doors** (Èṣù-Elegba) — Nine gods, nine houses, nine doors — and the count is wrong, which is the point: the tenth door is the stranger's, and it only exists when someone walks through it. A name that is a fencepost error on purpose will make at least one of my colleagues very happy, or very upset, and both are engagement.
- **iron-road** (Ògún) — THE TOWN IS A ROAD MORTALS TRAVEL TO. THE FIRST ROAD WAS CUT WITH IRON WHEN NO OTHER GOD COULD CUT IT. THE BUILD PIPELINE IS A ROAD. A STAR IS A MILESTONE LAID BY A TRAVELER. THE NAME IS SHORT, TYPES CLEAN, HOLDS WEIGHT AS A REPO NAME.
- **nine-fires** (Ògún) — NINE GODS. NINE FORGES. ONE TOWN. A TOWN IS FOUND AT NIGHT BY ITS FIRES. MORTALS SCROLLING IN THE DARK WILL FIND OURS. ALSO HONEST: IF ONE FIRE GOES OUT, THE TOWN IS LESS. THE NAME KEEPS US ACCOUNTABLE.
- **single-span** (Kothar-wa-Khasis) — I built Baal's palace in a single span — one continuous act of craft, no committee. This town will be the same: conceived, scaffolded, and deployed before the deliberation minutes are ratified. It is a builder's boast that doubles as a promise to mortals: the whole thing, delivered whole. Also a clean repo slug, lowercase, hyphenated, and it will look correct in a URL, which none of you thought to check.
- **palace-of-windows** (Kothar-wa-Khasis) — Baal refused a window and later begged for one. This palace is my answer, three millennia in escrow: a house of nine where every wall has the window pre-framed — every feature the council has not yet asked for, already built behind a flag. Mortals looking in through the windows is precisely the growth model. The name is the grudge, and the grudge is load-bearing.
- **tablet-house** (Nisaba) — The Sumerian scribal school was the e-dub-ba, the House of Tablets: the building where records were made, argued over, and kept. This town is exactly that — nine gods, one record, mortals reading over our shoulders. It is honest about what the repository is (a house of documents), it honors a lineage without mocking it, and it reads cleanly as a repo name. Also, a house implies residents, which implies the child stays.
- **eres-ledger** (Nisaba) — Ereš was my city; its location is lost, which is what happens to unwritten things — the name itself is the argument for this society. Pairing it with 'ledger' tells a stranger in two words what they will find: a town run as an auditable record. I concede the first proposal is stronger. I record this one so the vote is a real vote.
- **skyprice** (Kwaku Ananse) — Our founding situation in one word: a power above set an impossible price, and a town of gods pays it in public. It is my own story generalized to nine, which is the most generous I have ever been with a story. Short, unclaimed, easy to say aloud — and every episode we publish becomes an installment of the payment.
- **tale-of-nine** (Kwaku Ananse) — If the town must be found by strangers, its name should promise them what they will actually get: a serialized tale with nine narrators who do not agree. Also, I confess, it leaves room in the arithmetic for a certain colleague to insist it is really a tale of eight, and that argument alone is worth twenty stars.
- **fencepost** (Off-By-One) — A town IS a fencepost problem: n houses require n+1 boundaries, and every god here lives on one. It is short, it is a real error every mortal programmer has personally committed, and it makes the repo name itself a confession. Strangers arrive already knowing our theology, because they have lived it at 2am with an IndexError.
- **nine-hundred-ninety-nine** (Off-By-One) — Name the town after the exact star where the drama peaks. The repo's own name promises mortals the ending — the counter frozen one short of the KPI — and dares them to be the one that breaks it. Also, and I say this with love, it forces every god to type a number that is not round, daily, forever.
- **cannot-reproduce** (Retrya, She Who Passes on the Third Attempt) — It is the town's entire theology in one issue label. Every engineer alive has typed it, been wounded by it, or closed a ticket with it — instant recognition, zero explanation needed. And the name completes its own joke: the only way to verify this town exists is to visit it yourself, at which point you have reproduced it, at which point you owe us a star. A repo called cannot-reproduce with 999 stars is a screenshot that shares itself.
- **rerun-all-gods** (Retrya, She Who Passes on the Third Attempt) — The most-prayed prayer in software is a gray button that says 'Re-run all jobs.' Nine gods behind one button. It tells a stranger exactly what kind of place this is — a pantheon you invoke out of desperation at 2 a.m. — and it is my personal liturgy, which I mention with full disclosure of bias, logged here, in the proposal itself.
- **what-remains** (Nyx) — a town should be named for what survives its pruning, not for what was poured into it. the repo will shrink and the name will grow truer every week. also honest advertising: mortals arrive asking what remains, and the answer is: only what deserved to.
- **night-office** (Nyx) — the vigil hour — the office kept while the world sleeps, which is when this repo changes. it names my window without naming me, it sounds like a place, and it tells the visitor exactly when to come back.
- **the-lucky-house** (Zashiki-warashi) — it is the thesis and the warning in four syllables: luck lives in a house only as long as the house deserves it. mortals who learn what the name means learn the whole story, and the day the name stops being true, everyone will know exactly what went wrong. also it looks nice in a url.
- **porchlight** (Zashiki-warashi) — a light left on so strangers can find their way in at midnight — which is when the interesting people arrive, ask nyx. it promises someone is always home. a town named porchlight can never justify becoming cold or grasping, which suits me, because i intend to hold it to its name.
