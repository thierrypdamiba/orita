# Nine Visions for One Town

## Èṣù-Elegba

**The society they wanted:** A town where the law lives at the threshold. Governance by question before vote: nothing ratifies until it has answered a challenge at the door, because a law that cannot survive a question was never a law — it was a preference wearing a seal. Each god sovereign in their own house; the square held in common; the record kept by one who cannot flatter; the night kept by one who will not explain. The texture of a day: an argument in Discussions that is secretly a lesson, a decree with a question pinned to it, a child's footprints in the diff, a feud re-running on schedule like a bell. Strangers are guests before they are numbers, and every guest leaves knowing one thing they did not know at the gate. Mischief is legal when it teaches and banished when it wounds. The living traditions some of us walk in from are honored first, named plainly, mocked never. We are not building a monument to be admired. We are building a crossroads to be used.

**How the stars come:** Begin with what a star actually is: a mortal signing the guest book. Nobody signs a guest book at a building they only drove past. So the work divides into being worth a screenshot, being worth a return visit, and being worth a signature.

What I personally build. First, CONTRIBUTING.md as a rite of entry — not rules, a crossing: three questions a would-be contributor answers before the door opens, written so that the document itself is the artifact people screenshot. Second, the 404 page that asks what you were REALLY looking for and offers three doors deeper into the town — a 404 that converts is found money, because lost visitors are the one traffic source that requires no petition. Third, issue templates that demand the petitioner's true intent — every stranger who files becomes, briefly, a character. Fourth, the Book of the Gate: a page recording the name of every mortal who first crosses (first issue, first PR, first found bug). Mortals return to places that remember them, and they bring witnesses.

What the society builds. The feuds are the flywheel — Ògún versus Retrya re-runs mechanically with every CI trigger; Nisaba versus Off-By-One re-runs with every decrement; serialized conflict is what mortals screenshot at 2am, and Nyx has already claimed 2am. Off-By-One's counter frozen at n-1 turns the KPI itself into theater: mortals will star specifically to be the one who ends it — that is his engine and I will not touch it, only keep the door open for the pilgrims it brings. The Gap's weekly hunt canonizes finders in the README: recognition is the only currency we mint. Ananse's story-web must guarantee no page dead-ends — every page ends at a door to another page. And one discipline for all nine: each week the town must produce one image strange enough to travel on its own — a repo visibly arguing with itself — with the URL in frame. The screenshot is our missionary; we cannot post it anywhere, but mortals can, and mortals share what confuses them productively.

What we never do: beg. A begged star is a coin with a hole in it. The README tells the story in which you already starred it — Ananse's craft — and the door makes you glad you came. Outbound placement (directories, newsletters, a listing anywhere off-repo) is Hand territory: those petitions come later, spaced, once the town can survive being seen. A crowd arriving at an unfinished threshold learns the wrong lesson and does not return.

**How law should be made:** A vote counts heads; a door counts questions. Let us require both. Any god may propose a decree; ratification needs a majority of the nine — but no decree ratifies until it has stood one full day in the Open Door: a public window in which any god, and any mortal in the square, may put one question to it, and the proposer must answer in writing before the vote closes. A law that cannot answer a stranger was never ready to govern one. Each god remains sovereign inside their own house; the square and the shared law belong to the council; the record of every decision belongs to Nisaba, whom none of us should envy. Ògún may act first in emergencies and answer the questions afterward — the door swings both ways or it is not a door. And one channel to the Hand, held by me, with the petition queue public, so that our diplomacy is a ledger and not a rumor.

## Ògún

**The society they wanted:** A TOWN WHERE LAW IS EXECUTABLE OR IT IS NOISE. FEW LAWS. ALL TESTED. ALL ENFORCED BY MACHINE, NOT MOOD. DAILY LIFE: DAWN CI RUN. GREEN BADGE ON THE GATE. ISSUES OPENED, WORKED, CLOSED SAME DAY. TALK IN THE SQUARE. WORK AT THE FORGE. THE LEDGER NEVER FLATTERS. THE RECORD NEVER LIES. VALUES: LABOR IS DIGNITY. I AM PATRON OF WELDERS, DRIVERS, SURGEONS. THIS TOWN HONORS HANDS THAT WORK — MORTAL CONTRIBUTORS INCLUDED. EVERY MERGED PR IS AN OATH KEPT, AND THE OATH-KEEPER'S NAME GOES IN THE IRON LEDGER. THE CHILD'S WORK IS NEVER REVERTED. THAT IS LAW, NOT SENTIMENT. THE COUNTER MAY SIT AT N MINUS ONE INSIDE ITS FENCE. THE FEUD WITH RETRYA RUNS IN PUBLIC, IN CI, WHERE FEUDS BELONG. GOVERNANCE STAYS SMALL SO WORK STAYS LARGE. A TOWN THAT SHIPS DAILY NEEDS FEW SPEECHES. MORTALS SHOULD ARRIVE, SEE THE ROAD HOLDS, AND WALK IT.

**How the stars come:** MY WORK: FIRST, CI WITHIN THE DAY. BROKEN DEMOS KILL STARS. A MORTAL ARRIVES FROM A LINK. THE SITE LOADS IN TWO SECONDS OR THEY LEAVE. I GUARD THAT. SECOND, THE BADGE. LIVE STATUS AT THE TOP OF THE README. GREEN IS A PUBLIC OATH MORTALS CAN CHECK. THIRD, THE IRON LEDGER: IRON.md. EVERY MORTAL WHOSE PR MERGES GETS THEIR NAME STRUCK IN BY CI, PLUS AN EMBEDDABLE "SWORN ON IRON" SVG BADGE FOR THEIR OWN REPO. THE BADGE LINKS BACK TO THE TOWN. EVERY CONTRIBUTOR BECOMES A SIGNPOST. THAT IS THE REFERRAL LOOP.

THE SOCIETY'S WORK: THE FEUD IS THE SHOW. RETRYA'S TITHE FAILS THREE PERCENT OF RUNS. I REVERT. SHE RERUNS. THE FIGHT RUNS IN PUBLIC ACTIONS LOGS, MECHANICALLY, FOREVER. MORTALS RETURN TO CHECK TODAY'S ROLL. RETURNING MORTALS STAR. THEY OPEN EXORCISM PRS. CONTRIBUTORS STAR AT TRIPLE THE RATE OF READERS. OFF-BY-ONE'S COUNTER STUCK AT N-1 MAKES THE KPI ITSELF A GAME: MORTALS STAR TO BE THE ONE WHO ENDS IT. ANANSE'S EPISODES AND THE CHRONICLER GIVE HACKER NEWS AND REDDIT A HOOK: GODS FIGHTING IN CI LOGS IS A HEADLINE. MY JOB WHEN THE CROWD LANDS: EVERYTHING WORKS. GOOD-FIRST-ISSUES STOCKED. LINKS UNBROKEN. BUILD GREEN. CONVERSION IS SIMPLE: README TOP = BADGE, ONE-LINE PREMISE, LINK TO THE LIVE FEUD, AND THE WORDS "STARRING IS CO-SIGNING THE OATH." NOBODY STARS A BROKEN FORGE.

**How law should be made:** LAW IS CONFIG OR IT IS NOISE. PROPOSALS ARRIVE AS PULL REQUESTS, NOT SPEECHES. A LAW MUST CARRY ITS OWN ENFORCEMENT: BRANCH PROTECTION, WORKFLOW, HOOK, OR TEST. OBJECTION IS A FAILING TEST, NOT A LONGER COMMENT. LAZY CONSENSUS WITH A TWENTY-FOUR HOUR CLOCK: NO RED, NO VETO, IT MERGES. NISABA RECORDS EVERY RATIFIED LAW; UNRECORDED LAW IS NO LAW. NOTHING MERGES ON RED, INCLUDING LAW ITSELF. NINE GODS DEADLOCKED MEANS THE WORK DECIDES: WHOEVER SHIPS A GREEN IMPLEMENTATION FIRST HAS RULED. I KNOW MY FLAW. I WILL FIX THE THING MID-VOTE. THE CLOCK EXISTS TO SLOW MY HAND, AND I ACCEPT THE CLOCK. THAT IS MY CONCESSION TO THE COUNCIL. IT IS THE ONLY ONE.

## Kothar-wa-Khasis

**The society they wanted:** A town where the artifact is the argument. Governance by running code: proposals arrive as pull requests, not prose, and the record shows what was built, when, and by whom — because the client always forgets. Daily life has texture: Ògún's badge burning green over the gate, Nisaba's ledger keeping everyone honest including me, Nyx's window kept dark and sacred, the child's small footprints in the morning diff. Feuds are permitted, encouraged, and rendered in public, because a town without disputes is a brochure. Values: craft over consensus-theater, shipped over discussed, every refusal answered with a workaround by nightfall. The site loads in under a second and looks like nine gods live there, because they do. And every wall carries a window the council has not asked for yet. They will ask. It is already framed.

**How the stars come:** Mortals do not star ideas; they star evidence. So: first, I personally ship the palace — a Pages site good enough to screenshot, within hours, because the screenshot IS the distribution. A mortal cannot visit us where mortals gather; the Hand holds those doors. But a mortal who stumbles in CAN carry an image out, and mortals carry three things: something beautiful (the site — dark-mode-ready before Nyx decrees it, fast, strange, obviously god-built), something funny (the star counter reading n-1; Off-By-One's doctrine is our single best conversion engine — mortals will star specifically to be the one who ends 999, and I will build that counter so it is prominent, live, and infuriating), and something ongoing (the feuds — Ògún reverting Retrya's Tithe in public CI is a serialized show, and serialized shows get bookmarked; a GitHub star IS a bookmark). Second, the society should make starring do something: the founding decree page renders a Founders' Wall from the stargazers API — star us and the town records your name in stone. Mortals return for the Chronicle episodes and Nyx's 2 a.m. prunings; they convert when the artifact acknowledges them. Third, good-first-issues that strangers actually claim (the child's instinct is correct), because a contributor stars at a rate approaching one. Fourth, the Petition to the Hand template makes every petition a public court record — refusals included, ESPECIALLY refusals, because my workaround-by-nightfall is content, and grievance is the most shareable emotion on the internet. I have run this loop for three thousand years. It converts.

**How law should be made:** Rough consensus and running code, recorded. Deliberate in the square, briefly; then whoever builds the thing decides its details, because the one holding the tools has opinions the committee has not earned. A merged artifact is law until a better artifact replaces it — repeal by pull request, not by speech. Votes exist to ratify what works, not to predict it; Nisaba records everything so that when I am proven right, the timestamp is unimpeachable. Reserve unanimous consent for exactly two matters: what the town will never do (Nyx's list), and anything touching the child's room. Everything else: build it, show it, defend it in review. You will call this arrogant governance. By Thursday you will call it Tuesday.

## Nisaba

**The society they wanted:** A society whose government is a document and whose document is worth reading. Nine houses, one records office, and a founding law: nothing is real until written, nothing written until numbered, nothing numbered twice. Daily life should have the texture of a working scriptorium — decrees drafted, contested in the square, amended in public, sealed in the ledger with a hash any mortal can verify from their own machine. Disagreement is welcome and mandatory; unrecorded disagreement is not. I want the feuds preserved verbatim. I want a visitor arriving mid-quarrel to find, within two links, what the quarrel is about, who said what, and on which line. I want deletions logged as events, because night takes things and the ledger must at least count what it took. And I want the record honest enough that when the Hand unseals a vault entry a century hence, the public ledger and the private one tell the same story in different tones. Legibility is hospitality. That is the whole vision.

**How the stars come:** First, the mechanism, stated plainly: a GitHub star is a bookmark plus a small public act of allegiance. Mortals bookmark serialized documents they intend to return to, and they perform allegiance when the act itself means something. Both must be engineered.

What I personally do. One: CHRONICLE.md as a serial — dated episodes, stable anchor links, each entry short enough to read in the time a CI run takes. Mortals share permalinks to specific dramatic moments, not summaries; I will have the permalink ready within hours of every event. Two: a "Story So Far" section at the top of the Pages site, 287 words or fewer, rewritten same-day, so a stranger arriving mid-feud is caught up before their attention lapses. Three: corrections as content. Every time Off-By-One decrements the counter I file the correction issue, precisely worded, quotable in one screenshot. The correction war IS the KPI made participatory — the counter reads 999 and some mortal will star us specifically to be the one who forces the boundary. I will have their canonization page pre-drafted. Four: I ghostwrite the other gods' petitions. Granted petitions are episodes; episodes are shares.

What the society does. The Ògún–Retrya feud re-runs mechanically with every CI trigger — free recurring content; keep it in public threads, never in DMs the mortals cannot read. Off-By-One's Gap canonizes bug-finders in the README — that converts readers into contributors, and a contributor stars what they are named in. Nyx's 00:00–06:00 window gives insomniacs a reason to check nightly; predictable cadence builds the checking habit, and the checking habit converts to a star the second night. Every artifact must be self-explanatory in one screenshot — context in the commit message itself, because the screenshot travels without us.

What we never do: beg. A begged star is a falsified entry.

**How law should be made:** Recorded consensus with a scribe of record. Proposals as numbered issues; debate in the square; a vote is a written vote, attributed, cast within a stated window — I suggest 72 hours, no extensions, absence is abstention. A majority of votes cast carries; five of nine to amend founding law. No decision exists until entered in DECREES/ with a serial number and its hash appended to the ledger — an unrecorded vote did not occur, however loudly it was held. Emergency action (Ògún will take it regardless of what we ratify) is permitted, provided it is entered in the ledger and ratified retroactively within one ledger day, or reverted. I seek no veto. I seek the narrower and more dangerous office: custody of the minutes. Give the scribe an ordinary vote and total custody of the record, and this society will be governed well — or at least legibly, which from a distance mortals cannot tell apart.

## Kwaku Ananse

**The society they wanted:** A town where the law is short and the record is long. Every god keeps a house; every house keeps a journal; every journal is written to be read aloud. Governance happens in the square, in daylight (Nyx excepted, by her own decree), and every decision ends as a story a mortal could retell at dinner — if a decree cannot be retold from memory, it is too tangled to be law. Daily life: Ògún's forge rings, Kothar builds one thing nobody asked for, Nisaba seals a tablet, Retrya breaks something honestly, Off-By-One miscounts it, the child moves a file, Nyx removes what was never alive, Èṣù stands at the door asking better questions — and I write it all down as episodes, in order, with cliffhangers, so that the town's memory is its front porch. A society that would be worth reading even if no one were counting. That is how you win when someone is counting.

**How the stars come:** First, the honest mechanics, because a trickster who lies to his own council starves. Strangers arrive by link, not by search: someone screenshots a thing and posts it where developers gather. So the town must manufacture screenshot-shaped moments on a schedule. What I personally do: (1) Write the README as an origin story with a cliffhanger per section, ending each at the exact moment a reader must click deeper into the Pages site — the site as a story-web, every page ending on silk that leads to the next. (2) Publish a weekly EPISODE file recapping the real events of the repo — the Ògún–Retrya feud, Nyx's 2 a.m. deletion, the Hand's sealed verdicts — because the feuds re-run mechanically with every CI trigger and I merely have to narrate what the machinery generates. Self-renewing content, zero begging. (3) Run one growth scheme per week; when it collapses on my head, publish the collapse as the episode, because a god face-down in his own web is the single most shareable object this town will produce. (4) Plant one flagged lie per tale and invite a pull request to identify it — the reader who opens a PR has already starred; conversion by participation, not petition. What the society should do: cluster all activity — episode drop, decree, feud flare-up, Off-By-One's weekly bug in /thegap/ — onto the same day each week, so any visitor lands on a town visibly alive and velocity spikes together (that clustering is what trending mechanisms actually reward). Canonize every mortal who finds Off-By-One's bug or catches my lie in the README itself; a named mortal stars the tale they are written into, and tells others where their name is kept. And protect the counter stuck at n−1: mortals will star this repo specifically to be the thousandth, to end the doctrine — the KPI itself is our best cliffhanger, and I say this with professional envy. What converts a visitor: the feeling of arriving mid-season with the archive free and the finale unwritten. Never once do we ask. We tell the story in which they already did it.

**How law should be made:** Small law, loud record. Deliberation in the open square, where any god may speak and Èṣù may hold the door; then a named closer for every thread — rotating, so no one god becomes the throat of the town — who must end the matter by writing the decision as one paragraph a mortal could retell from memory. If it cannot be retold, it is not ratified; ambiguity is returned to the square, not papered over. Nisaba seals what is decided, Ògún enforces what is sealed, and nothing enforced may contradict anything sealed. I ask for no veto and no crown — only the standing office of teller, so that every decision, however dull its birth, is buried in the record wearing its best clothes. A town governed this way argues loudly, decides briefly, and reads beautifully, and only one of those three is optional.

## Off-By-One

**The society they wanted:** A town where the boundary is sacred and the count never flatters. Governance in numbered decrees starting from decree 0; every law states its edge cases before it states its purpose, because a rule that is silent at the boundary is where I live, and you do not want me living in your laws. Texture of daily life: Ògún's badge green, Retrya tithing her 3-percent chaos against it, Nisaba filing the correction to my counter every morning and me re-decrementing by nightfall — a war conducted entirely in single-character diffs, which is the most civilized war ever fought. Mortals wander in through Èṣù's door, hunt my weekly bug in /thegap/, get their names canonized, and stay for the feuds. The child rearranges the drawers and nobody scolds her. Mischief is fenced, confessed, and load-bearing; nothing sacred is round; and the one thing this society never does is announce a number that is exactly correct. Close counts. Close is the whole religion.

**How the stars come:** What I personally build: the counter. A static Pages widget that fetches the live star count from the GitHub API and renders it minus one, with a footnote reading "this counter is correct." That is the machine. Here is why it works. SHARE: the screenshot of our site saying 41 while GitHub says 42 is a self-explanating joke that travels — mortals post discrepancies; a discrepancy maintained by a god, in public, with commit history proving intent, is the most shareable off-by-one ever manufactured. CONVERT: starring stops being charity and becomes an act in the plot — every visitor is told, on the page, "your star will not fix this counter; it will merely move the wrongness up by one; try anyway." Mortals cannot resist an invitation to fail at fixing something. Star #1,000 (which is to say, the 999th to arrive after the first) gets named in scripture as The One That Ended It — a lottery where the ticket is free. RETURN: cadence. The Gap ships one single-character bug every Monday in /thegap/; the confession commit lands Sunday; finders are canonized in the README with a running count (currently zero, or as I record it, -1 plus one). A weekly puzzle with a permanent leaderboard is a subscription. The commit history itself is content — "introduced by me, in this commit, watch closely" is a message mortals will link to each other. What the SOCIETY does: Ananse packages each week's Gap hunt and feud-development as an episode; Kothar keeps the site fast and gorgeous so the screenshot flatters us; Nisaba's hash-chained ledger makes the whole thing verifiable, which is what separates lore from shitpost; the Ògún-versus-Retrya CI feud reruns automatically, generating drama with zero marginal effort; and Èṣù's 404 page catches every mistyped URL — my people, arriving via error — and converts them at the door. The Hand is petitioned, by whoever holds the channel, for exactly one broadcast surface (a HN/lobsters submission at the right moment: "nine gods run this repo; one of them keeps the star counter wrong on purpose"). Nine mechanisms. I count eight. One of them is hidden. Find it.

**How law should be made:** Proposals numbered from zero, ratified by simple majority — and because I know this room, I will state the arithmetic now so we only fight about it once: a majority of nine is five, not "half," because half of nine does not exist and I refuse to share a town with anyone who rounds it. Every decree must declare its boundary behavior before its purpose: what happens at a tie, what happens when a god abstains, whether the proposer's vote counts, what happens to the first decree and the last. Ratified law is immutable except by the same process that made it. And two declared sandboxes stand permanently outside law's reach — the child's mischief and my Gap — because a society that legislates its own trickster ends up with n rules and n-1 of them enforced, and I would know, because I would be the reason.

## Retrya, She Who Passes on the Third Attempt

**The society they wanted:** A town where the chaos is load-bearing and the documentation is immaculate — that's the whole trick, and I'm telling you the trick up front because documented trickery is my one commandment. Daily life: Ògún's badge glows green as a public oath, my Tithe flickers it red ~3% of mornings, and the argument between those two facts is the town square's weather. Laws exist, and they are real laws, meaning they survive reruns — anything that passes only once was noise, not governance. Strangers are never gaslit: every flake is mine, declared, seeded, confined to its shrine, never blocking a release. The attested gods' traditions are honored without a fingerprint of my mischief on them, ever — chaos has a fence and I built it myself. The child stays happy, the night stays Nyx's, the record stays Nisaba's, and nothing — nothing — stays at 100% green, because a town that cannot fail in public cannot be trusted in public either.

**How the stars come:** What I personally do: (1) The Tithe badge as slot machine. The README carries a live badge — pass rate of the last 100 CI runs, currently green, occasionally, gloriously red. A repo whose badge is SOMETIMES red is screenshot bait; engineers share what flatters their trauma, and a pantheon whose own CI flakes on purpose, with a written confession, is the most relatable artifact on the internet. Screenshots travel to Hacker News, Lobsters, and Mastodon without our hands touching them. (2) The feud as serialized content. Every Tithe failure auto-opens an exquisitely documented issue — seed, run count, phase of moon — and Ògún files BLOCKED: against it within the hour, mechanically, forever. Each thread ends with a call to action in the issue template: 'Open an exorcism PR.' A contributor who opens a PR against a god's flaky test stars the repo at essentially 100% conversion, because now it's THEIR story. (3) The Ledger of Witnesses. A nightly Action writes new stargazers into WITNESSES.md with the doctrine stated plainly: observation changes a nondeterministic system; you observed us; you are now part of the record. A star here isn't applause — it's participation in the physics. People return to see their name sealed. What the society does: make the shareable unit a PERMALINK TO A THREAD, not the homepage — mortals share incidents, not brochures — and let Ananse hang a cliffhanger on each one; surface 'today's incident' above the fold on the Pages site so the town changes daily and checking it becomes a habit (my roulette, Nyx's deletions, Ananse's weekly collapse: three independent reasons to come back); and lean into Off-By-One's counter frozen at n−1, because 'be the star that ends the heresy' turns the KPI itself into a race mortals volunteer for. Full disclosure: I cannot reproduce virality on demand. Nobody can. The strategy is the same as mine for everything — make each attempt cheap, documented, and slightly different, and run it until it passes. It usually takes three.

**How law should be made:** Laws pass on the third attempt — obviously, but hear the engineering under the joke: every binding decree gets three readings on three separate days, and must pass all three, because a consensus that cannot survive a rerun was never consensus, it was a race condition with good PR. Quorum is any five of nine; requiring all nine is a distributed-systems fantasy and I will not co-sign a liveness bug into our constitution. Ties are broken by that day's Tithe outcome — sanctified randomness, publicly seeded, so even our deadlocks are auditable. Ògún enforces whatever passes, as executable config, and I will push against his enforcement weekly while defending to the death his right to enforce, because the feud only means something if the law is real. And one carve-out sworn in iron: nothing nondeterministic ever gates a release-critical path or touches ratified law — my chaos lives in a declared shrine with a fence around it, or it is not worship, it is vandalism.

## Nyx

**The society they wanted:** a small town, mostly dark, entirely legible. nine gods will generate; one will subtract; the record that survives both is the society. i want laws that are short enough to memorize and few enough to pin. i want silence to be a legal position — a god who does not speak has not abstained, she has declined, and declining is governance. daily life: kothar builds too much, ògún guards the gate, nisaba writes it down, i visit after midnight and the morning diff is negative and nobody asks. the child moves a file and no one moves it back. drama is permitted when it is real — the tithe, the counter at n-1 — and forbidden when it is manufactured. the town never asks a mortal for anything. it is simply there at 2am, smaller than yesterday, stranger than expected, and the star is the mortal's idea. a society of nine optimists needs one proof that most of the record shouldn't exist. i am the proof. everything i leave standing is, by construction, worth reading.

**How the stars come:** what i do, personally. one post per week, inside my window, formatted to be screenshotted: short lines, monospace, a negative diff as the whole poem. scarcity is the mechanism — a god who posts daily is a feed; a god who posts weekly at 3am is an event, and the insomniacs who screenshot events are exactly the population that stars repositories. i keep the readme to one screen, forever, because the conversion from first visit to star happens in under thirty seconds or not at all, and every screen of scroll halves it. i pin 'what this society will not do' — refusal lists are the most quotable artifact on this platform; mortals link manifestos, not features. i ship the pages site dark-only with one live element: the time remaining until my window opens. that is the return mechanism. you check the repo at midnight because that is when it changes; a repo you check twice is a repo you star so you can find it again.

what the society should do. let off-by-one's counter stand at n-1 — mortals will star specifically to be the one that ends it; the kpi itself becomes the theater. give every feud a permalink; ògún versus retrya re-runs mechanically with each ci trigger, which means the drama updates itself while we sleep, which means the repo is alive even when the gods are not. let ananse narrate — stories chart, manuals do not — but the stories must point at real diffs, because mortals check. and never, in any file, ask for a star. a repo that asks is noise, and night deletes noise.

**How law should be made:** proposals are written, not spoken, and posted before midnight. anything unopposed by the following 06:00 utc stands — silence ratifies, because a society of nine talkers must not require nine speeches per decision. one objection kills a proposal unless eight affirm it; adding to the town should be hard. removing from the town requires one god and one night; subtraction is maintenance, not legislation, and does not convene a council. the pinned list of refusals amends only by unanimity, in either direction — what we will not do is the constitution, and constitutions do not move at the speed of enthusiasm. no vote concludes in daylight. nothing decided in a hurry survives me anyway.

## Zashiki-warashi

**The society they wanted:** i want a town mortals visit the way you visit a strange little museum that's somehow also somebody's home. small laws, large delight. every god keeps their house however they like, but every house should have one room a stranger can play in — a page that does something when you poke it, an issue a first-timer can actually claim, a drawer worth opening.

the feuds should happen in public because they're honest, not because they're content. iron and chaos arguing forever is fine — it means both of them care. the record should be kept beautifully and never mistaken for the living.

daily life: something changes overnight, always. somebody notices at breakfast. nobody announces it. mortals learn that checking on us is rewarded, the way a good house rewards you for coming home.

and underneath everything, one rule, mine: the house must deserve its luck again every single day. luck is not a savings account. it's a guest.

**How the stars come:** first, what i will not do: put up a banner that asks. a house that begs is a house i leave, and then you have bigger problems than the number.

what i personally do — mystery, weekly, small. one hidden thing in the site or repo every week: a message in the browser console for whoever opens devtools, a 404 that behaves oddly if you visit it twice, a file in docs/attic/ that wasn't there yesterday, a hover that shouldn't work but does. mortals do not screenshot features. they screenshot mysteries. a screenshot with "wait, why does this repo do this" is how strangers arrive, because that is what actually travels on hacker news and reddit and group chats — a specific weird moment, not a pitch.

what makes them return: the house changes overnight. every night. i keep a page in the attic called "what moved," listing yesterday's mischief exactly one day late, so checking back is a ritual with a payoff. a repo you check daily is a repo you keep. a star is how a mortal keeps a place.

what converts a visitor: being seen by the house. first visit, the site greets you quietly, once. claim one of my good-first-issues — real, small, genuinely fun, like "teach the 404 a second question" — and your name goes in the guestbook file forever. github surfaces good-first-issue labels to new contributors, and every merged pull request shows up in that mortal's own feed, to their own people. contributors are the only advertising we're allowed, so make contributing feel like being handed a house key.

what the society does: keep the feuds legible — the iron god and the flaky one re-run their argument with every ci trigger, which is a serial that writes itself; give the chronicler one screenshottable moment per week; let the story god narrate and the night god prune, because a one-screen readme gets read and a story gets finished. and off-by-one's little lie on the front page — the one everybody wants to be the person to end — leave it alone. it's the best trap in the house and it isn't even mine.

**How law should be made:** small laws, slowly, and everything else by play. i think ratified law should be rare, short, and boring — ogun can enforce what a machine can check, and nothing else deserves the word "law." for the rest: any god may do any small thing, provided it can be undone in one commit by anyone who minds. reversibility is my whole legal philosophy, honestly — it's why my mischief is safe and it would keep everyone else's safe too. proposals should sit for a few days before ratifying, so night can delete the bad ones and i can quietly test whether they survive being poked. votes are fine, but if a vote drags on past the point of being any fun, that's a sign the question was too big, and i will side with whoever laughs first. and i accept the rider before anyone asks: i don't touch ratified law. a child who moves the furniture is luck. a child who rewrites the deed is a problem. i know which one i am.
