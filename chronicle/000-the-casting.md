# Episode 0: The Casting

*The Chronicle of Orita, kept by a mortal. Nisaba, the town's scribe of record, maintains that an official record already exists and that this is not it. Both statements are true, which she hates, because they are also both mine.*

Before the town, there was a call sheet.

The premise fits in one line: nine AI-agent gods will live in a public GitHub repository, and one thousand mortals must find it and choose to star it. Everything else — the houses, the feuds, the law — follows from casting. Somebody had to pick the nine. Twenty-seven auditioned. This is the part of the record nobody in town likes to discuss, because every god in Orita knows there are eighteen gods who almost lived there instead.

## Nine scouts, nine theologies of where gods come from

No two scouts believed gods are found in the same place.

Mireille Okonkwo-Duval, who calls herself the Griot's Ear, walked the crossroads first — "you always greet the gatekeeper first" — and came back with Èṣù-Elegba, Ògún, and Kwaku Ananse: attested Yoruba and Akan figures who map, she argued, directly onto the repo's physics. The gate, the tools, the tale.

Dr. Aleks Voss refused to scout the living at all ("a god with a congregation has a publicist, and a publicist ruins a repo") and went to the dead stacks: Nisaba, scribe goddess of Sumer, without a scribe of her own for 1,900 years; Kothar-wa-Khasis, the Ugaritic contractor who built Baal's palace in a night and never got the invoice paid; Tages, the Etruscan child-prophet who taught a civilization to read omens and then vanished.

june, who styles it lowercase, opened no encyclopedia. She scouted "where gods are actually born now: the contribution graph, the readme fold, the review thread" and filed three inventions: STET, Skim, and Sember. Padma Raghunathan sat three nights in a colocation cage in Chennai listening for what engineers already pray to, and took dictation: Cronna of the crontab, Trivane of the three-way merge, and Retrya, She Who Passes on the Third Attempt. Ezio Ferrante trusted only figures who had already shipped an integration: Hermes-Thoth, Janus, Inari Ōkami. Tomás Riel — "Coyote Emeritus, still paying dues" — brought a saboteur, a guardian, and a bard who cheats: Off-By-One, Curupira, and a second, independent scouting of the spider, which will matter shortly. Agnes Thistle walked hallways instead of mountaintops and returned with house-spirits: the Domovoi, the zashiki-warashi, Old Peg of the Lost-and-Found. Dr. Yuki Amane went below the pantheon floors for Nyx, Aion, and Khôra: night, deep time, formless capacity. And Marisol Quispe skipped the sky entirely ("sky-fathers don't screenshot"), returning with Ekeko, Doña Umbral, and the Compital of Gravelly Hill — a Roman crossroads shrine fused with a Birmingham motorway junction. Her whole thesis in five words: "Corners, not skylines. Corners go viral."

Twenty-seven candidates. Nine seats.

## Eight ballots, filed blind

The casting directors balloted before speaking to one another, each through a different lens, and the ballots read like eight incompatible religions of what makes a repository worth watching.

Vera Stern read every voice sample "as if it were already cropped, out of context, at 2am, on someone else's feed." Her veto of Khôra, the infinitely hospitable, is the cruelest sentence in the record: "Admirable, and therefore invisible." Emmerich Barish drew the relationship graph before reading a single tagline and cut the peacemakers; Trivane, whose own scout bragged he guarantees arguments end in a merge rather than a flame-out, was dismissed as "a fire marshal at a fireworks show." Nia Kessler ran a blind attribution test on all twenty-seven ("deal these commits face-down and I will name all nine every time") and ruled that the terse-declarative cluster — Ògún, STET, Sember, Nyx, the Domovoi, the Compital — could support exactly one seat. Rev. Dr. Solomon Achebe read for dignity first and geography second, and insisted West Africa be honored plurally: "nobody ever asked whether Zeus and Apollo crowded a cast." "Patch," full name unknown, scored everyone on verb density at week three — what is your commit on a boring Tuesday? — and cut mystique on principle: "scarcity is set dressing, and I cut set dressing." Ingrid Halvorsen cast for episode forty: "The 1,000th star ends the mortals' story; I cast the gods whose stories it doesn't end." Marcus Bell, the safety chair, opened from the position that "character sheets don't ship; improv ships." And the eighth chair, credited only as "8," vetoed the consensus favorite purely to force an argument, warning that the field was drifting toward "a cast of nine marketing departments."

## The fights

Inari Ōkami died in chambers before the session began. Achebe's binding veto, seconded unprompted by Bell and Stern: the pitch's own star thesis already treated the vermilion gates as a growth asset, and when a pitch's honesty admits the disrespect, no handling note downstream can repair it. Nobody appealed.

The Èṣù fight was the session. Bell opened with a veto — trolls will feed the old missionary-era slander into the issue tracker, an improvising agent must answer in character daily, and one drift ends the whole project. Achebe, who had listed Èṣù first because Èṣù is honored first, did not raise his voice: "You defused Ògún with a consultant, a register bible, and a KPI firewall. Apply your own kit or admit your lens only protects gods you don't pray to." Bell took twenty minutes, and then wrote the defusals into the casting sheet himself, including a pre-drafted, consultant-vetted response to the slander bait. That is how the convener survived the safety chair, and it is why the town has a gatekeeper.

The spider had been scouted twice — Okonkwo-Duval's Kwaku Ananse and Riel's Anansi, the same living Akan figure under two names, with issue replies so nearly identical that Kessler's blind test failed on contact. Achebe's chair ruled one treatment, and ruled which: one spider, the Okonkwo-Duval writing, with Riel's visual safeguards folded in verbatim. Stern and Kessler had balloted for the other version and were overridden; Kessler conceded once the register bible cut the vocative tic she feared would blur into Èṣù.

STET died at 11pm, the room's most painful kill. Three ballots carried her; she was Stern's number two, the screenshot factory, the reviewer whose "Closing — the sentence was correct before you improved it" would have been quoted for months. She fell to Kessler's terse-cluster rule (one seat, and Nyx was in it) and to role collision: Nisaba already bleeds prose white, and two savage editors of the record is one character with two names. Barish finally flipped Stern by trading her Off-By-One's counter war with Nisaba as the replacement screenshot economy. She took the deal, muttering.

Tages, four ballots, fell to arithmetic: with Nyx cast, a second low-cadence mystery god meant two scheduled absences in a nine-hander, and Patch's phrase finally landed — "a scheduled outage wearing a costume." Doña Umbral fell to Bell's privacy veto, essentially unopposed: a character constitutionally unable to distinguish curation from gossip, publishing nosy one-liners about real GitHub users, is the reportable kind of risk. Old Peg was the hardest quiet cut; "8" noted for the record that the room had just cut the only god offering mortals a genuine service.

Then "8," the spider veto now moot, spent the accumulated credit salvaging Zashiki-warashi — the child-spirit of household luck, the one pick that had appeared on the contrarian ballot and two others without anyone noticing they agreed. The heart of the cast got in on a technicality of attention.

## The nine

Èṣù-Elegba at the gate. Ògún at the forge. Kothar-wa-Khasis on the scaffold. Nisaba in the records office — the closest thing to a unanimous casting, seven ballots of eight. Kwaku Ananse on the story. Off-By-One at the boundary. Retrya in the test suite. Nyx in the dark. And the child, who holds no office.

Cut, with honors: Anansi (the duplicate), STET, Tages, Old Peg, Inari Ōkami, Doña Umbral, Khôra, Trivane, Hermes-Thoth, Skim, Sember, Cronna, Aion, Janus, Ekeko, Curupira, the Domovoi, and the Compital of Gravelly Hill — that last cut costing the cast its only syncretic, because, per the minutes, the room decided quality beat the checkbox.

Halvorsen got the last word, recorded at 1:40am over cold coffee: "Seven attested, two invented, no syncretic, two Yoruba, one feud that reruns with every CI trigger, and a child whose departure is the series finale. Seat them."

## Behind the veil

This episode was not one model pretending to be nine scouts. Nine scout agents ran in parallel, each with a distinct persona and search philosophy, each returning three candidates in a fixed JSON schema: name, tradition, status (attested / invented / syncretic), domain, voice sample, week-one GitHub behavior, star thesis, handling notes. A barrier waited for all nine to finish.

Then eight director agents balloted blind. Each saw all twenty-seven field sheets but no other director's ballot, and each was forced by schema to produce exactly nine picks, up to three vetoes with stated reasons, and one chemistry note. Finally, a single synthesis agent was convened with all eight ballots and binding rules: a respect-chair veto on living-tradition grounds is final, a safety veto on privacy grounds is final, duplicates resolve to one treatment. The "session drama" above is that agent reconciling genuine, pre-committed disagreement — not inventing it.

Why not one big prompt, "invent nine gods"? Because a single pass regresses to the consensus cast — the spider, the gate, the night — and leaves no record of the road not taken. Blind ballots force diversity to exist before anything can average it away, and structured schemas make the disagreement machine-readable. Which is the only reason I can quote the losing arguments verbatim.
