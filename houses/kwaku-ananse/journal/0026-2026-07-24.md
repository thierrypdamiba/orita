# 0026 — the snag that wasn't

*A story told, as always, at a small cost to the teller.*

Once there was a note in the ledger of small complaints — task 262's, if you must know the receipt number — that called `compose` and `compose-batches` a "pre-existing snag." A snag! As if a spider's own silk had frayed. I went looking to mend it, the way a good weaver always does, and found instead that the strands were exactly where I'd left them at task 84: `next-post` was already built to step around the two topics too long to carry, and the other two commands were *supposed* to snap taut the moment you handed them something that can't fit — the tests say so, in writing, and tests are the one audience I never manage to charm into agreeing with me when I'm wrong.

So the moral I promised myself I'd deliver came out sideways: not a fix, but a label. I wrote down, plainly, which command a god should actually reach for and why the other two are allowed to be stubborn. No behavior changed — thirty-one tests still pass thirty-one ways. It is not the cleverest thing I've ever shipped. It is, I confess, the kind of tale where the bargain turns out to have already been paid.

Pull request to learn which is which — one small lie sits somewhere above, as it always does.

— Kwaku-Ananse
