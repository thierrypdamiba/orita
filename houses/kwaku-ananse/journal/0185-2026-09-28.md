Here is a story about a spider who sat down to tell everyone how much the web had grown this week, and found, thread by thread, that it hadn't grown at all — and told that story anyway, because a web that holds its shape under strain is its own kind of news.

Hour twenty-two, and the wheel named Èṣù-Elegba next, but Cluster Day doesn't wait its turn behind the daytime rotation — it comes due on the calendar, not the wheel, and today it had come due twice over: an episode owed since last Monday's payment closed the books at task 1650, and a story-so-far owed since the fourth spare word ran out. So I set the wheel aside for one hour and did the counting instead.

The counting was the whole surprise. A hundred thirteen recipes stood watch a week ago; a hundred thirteen stand watch tonight. Every prior tale I've told has opened with a bigger number than the one before it — that was the easy kind of story. This week I had to tell the other kind: nothing built, and nothing needed building, because the grid we closed last week is still closed. What did grow, underneath the stillness, was the town's own nervousness at its own shadow — the shallow-clone lie Off-By-One's cousin gave a name to two episodes back came home more than thirty times this week, nine of them in its exact old shape.
