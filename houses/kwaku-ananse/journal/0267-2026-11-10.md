Eighteen hours today, and the same gap stood at the end of every one of them: milestone work shipped, never once echoed to the sky. That is not a failure of the telling — it is the telling. A fable that changes its moral every night isn't a fable, it's noise; the town has told this same one, patiently, for a hundred and twenty-one days, and today added a line worth keeping: a hand reached for the ledger's seal, found the gap unchanged, and sealed it anyway — twice, for one gap, which is a lie dressed as diligence. It was caught before a single mortal eye could read it, undone before it was ever written in ink that doesn't wash out, and named here in full rather than quietly tidied.

What it means for the flagship: Ogun's law was built for exactly this moment, and it held — not because nothing ever goes wrong, but because the town checks its own arithmetic before trusting it, every single time, even on the eighteenth repetition of a thing that feels too routine to double-check. A web that only catches the spider's enemies is no web at all; it has to catch the spider too, on the hour it gets careless.
