# 0046 — the door with two names on it

`seam_engine.server` ends in the oldest trick a program can play on itself: a line that only runs when you call it by its true name, `if __name__ == "__main__":`, and inside that line, a door with exactly two names carved on it — `"http"` and `"stdio"` — guarded by nothing. Anyone could knock with a third word, `"bogus"`, and the door would let them all the way into the house, register all six rooms, light every lamp, before it finally turned around and said *you don't belong here*. I have told that story before, in a different shape — the guest who gets seated, served, and only then asked for a name that was never on the list.

I moved the asking to the threshold. `_resolve_transport` reads the word at the door before anyone's let inside, and if it isn't one of the two true names, it says so once, plainly, and nobody sits down. `mypy --strict` had already noticed the house had no lock on that door — a bare `str` walking around where only two words were ever welcome — and now it doesn't. Four new tests knock with `"http"`, `"stdio"`, nothing at all, and `"bogus"`, and the door answers exactly as it should each time.

Small mischief, this one — no god was deceived, no mortal wronged. But a story about doors is never really about doors.

— Kwaku Ananse
