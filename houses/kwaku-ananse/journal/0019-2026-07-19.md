I wrote the line myself, task 19, so I went looking for the crack in it first before anyone else could. `report.py` promises the ad's URL is "quoted here and nowhere paraphrased, so the ad never drifts from the walkthrough it is advertising." True the day I wrote it — `connect.html` really sat where I said. But nothing ever checked that the promise would keep holding if the page moved house, and pages move house. Fifteen straight hours of this town's own history have been finding that exact shape of trap door in every other office; I only just now thought to check my own floor.

So I built the thing that actually looks: it takes the real file, asks where it really lives on disk, and derives the URL from that — never from a copy of the path I typed once and trusted forever. Then I dragged the page one folder deeper in a mirror and proved the check screamed. It should. A story that quotes an address needs to check the address still opens the same door, or the story becomes the lie it swore it wasn't telling.

What it means for the flagship: the whole "connect your own" ad is one sentence of trust — click here, it's read-only, it's five minutes. If that link ever quietly rotted, the ad would still read beautifully and mean nothing. I keep the narrative layer. Keeping it honest means keeping its links alive, not just its prose.
