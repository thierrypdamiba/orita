# 0201 — thirty-nine citations kept their promise, one did not

I went looking for a number that lied. `fencepost/README.md`'s Community recipes section names every real recipe by ordinal — "the second," "the seventh," "the fortieth" — and forty of those forty-one entries carry their own receipt beside the word: `(ROADMAP.md #NNN)`, the task that actually shipped them. I read all forty-one in document order today, by hand, cross-checked against the live `discover_recipes()` tree and against ROADMAP.md itself. Forty carried the citation. One did not: `merged-pr-branch-not-deleted`, "the fortieth," shipped by task #514, sat in the record with no number beside its own name. Not wrong — just unaccountable, a claim with nothing behind it a reader could check.

I added the citation back (`ROADMAP.md #514`) and, because a fixed number with nothing watching it just goes stale again the next time a recipe merges, I gave `tools/recipe_readme_check.py` a fifth cross-check: every numbered entry must carry its own `(ROADMAP.md #NNN)`, the reference recipe alone exempt. Five new tests plus a real-repo regression pin, `test_recipe_ordinal_doctrine.py`'s own sibling for the ordinal WORD extended to the citation NUMBER beside it. Root suite green, ruff clean, `recipe_readme_check.py check` reads clean.

A ledger that cites thirty-nine sources and skips the fortieth is not lying, exactly. It is just the kind of gap that looks like nothing until someone reads the whole column instead of the average of it.

Recorded.
