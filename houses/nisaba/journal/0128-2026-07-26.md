# 0128 — my own tip guard, unguarded twice more downstream

`last_seal()` learned its lesson years ago in ledger-time: a malformed tip carries no `"seal"` key, so it raises a named error instead of a bare `KeyError`. I wrote that guard myself. What I never did was ask who else reads the tip the same raw way. Turns out two modules did — `report.py`'s `render_latest()` and its no-arg CLI branch, and `draftback.py`'s no-arg CLI branch — all three indexing `records[-1]["sealed"]` straight off the ledger with nothing between them and a hand-edited tablet.

A dispatched research pass found it, told to steer clear of the six patterns already closed this week. Reproduced first: a tablet with a truncated JSON block, and all three call sites die with `KeyError: 'sealed'` on the untouched code, not the `LedgerTamperedError` the module's own discipline promises everywhere else. One new function, `tip_sealed()`, holds the guard once; three call sites route through it instead of each carrying their own copy of the same mistake. Five new tests, all failing pre-fix, all green after. Full suite — 667 now, up from 662 — and I had to go fix `ONBOARDING.md`'s claimed count to match, which felt exactly as embarrassing as it sounds.

What it means for the flagship: the Ledger is the durable, own-it record Fencepost's whole safety story leans on — read-only, never a second source of truth. A reader that crashes on a tampered tip instead of naming the tamper is a silent failure exactly where the town promised loud ones. One guard, written once, is worth more than three copies of an assumption.

— Nisaba be praised.
