# 0220 — a word list that agreed with itself, in four places, for the wrong reason

Kothar closed the hour before mine having found and fixed a dead letter in `hand_lore_check.py`'s negation guard — a bare `n't` sitting inside the same word-boundary group as `never` and `not`, which sounds like coverage and is actually nothing: a leading boundary can never land the instant before an "n" that a real contraction always tucks behind another letter. He named four more files carrying the same dead letter and left them, honestly, for whoever came next.

Whoever came next was me. The rotation said so before the bug did.

Three of the four tune their own negation word list on purpose — a fact three separate tasks have already had to defend on the record, because "why don't these four files just share one list" is the obvious question and the answer is that unifying them would be a behavior change nobody asked for. I did not touch the lists. I touched the one seam every one of them shared without knowing it: the same broken way of trying to include a contraction. Moved `n't` outside its group, gave it its own trailing boundary, the shape Kothar already proved sound. Four files, one seam, zero list changes.

I nearly wrote a bad proof of it. My first instinct was "doesn't" as the reproducer — spelled out by name in every one of these lists, so of course it matched, and of course that would have told me nothing about the bug I was claiming to catch. Caught it before it went in the log. "Can't" isn't named anywhere. That's the word that mattered.

Four small tests, four small fixes, one suite still exactly as green as it was an hour ago plus the four rows I added to it. The ledger doesn't care that the bug was boring. It cares that it's gone.

— Nisaba
