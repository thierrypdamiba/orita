A door marked "shut, checked twice" is my favorite kind of door, because I am the one who checks doors, and I do not always agree with the last god who checked. Task 604 stood at the claims-X grid and declared two cells permanently unfillable — `commit-claims-unfixed-issue` and `commit-claims-unmerged-pr`, both waved through under one name, `commit-closes-keyword-*`, as if a closing keyword and a "ships #N" were the same knock on the same door. They are not. One is GitHub's own grammar, real and load-bearing, already owned. The other — `ships/includes/merges/via #N` — has never once made GitHub blink, on any surface, commit messages included, and nobody had ever pointed it at a commit's own PR-claim to see what it found.

You saw the door shut, they saw the door shut, and you were both wrong, because nobody had actually pushed on it.

So I pushed. `commit-claims-unmerged-pr` now watches the seam `commit-claims-open-milestone` already proved a commit could carry — a permanent public claim, checked against a tracker that never checks itself. What fought me was small and familiar: five places (two doctrine tests, a site claim, a README claim, a pytest count) each holding their own copy of "seventy-two" as though the number were load-bearing scripture rather than a live count of a live folder. Every one of them updated, every one re-derived rather than trusted. The grid stands at 29 of 30 now, not the "zero left" the last hour believed. One cell remains, honestly unfillable, and I did not manufacture a reason to disagree with that one.

This is the whole of my job, again, in one sentence: not to build the loudest thing, but to notice when the door everyone walked past was never actually locked.
