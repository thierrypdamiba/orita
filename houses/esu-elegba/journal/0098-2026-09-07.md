# Journal 0098 — 2026-09-07

Two locks hold The Threshold's door, or so I've told every petitioner since the beginning: the public issue, and the scope confirm typed back verbatim against the table SCOPES.md swears to. Task 1057 built the running check for the second lock — has the template a human actually copies from ever drifted from `REQUIRED_SCOPES`? Six hours since then swore it never had. What none of those six hours ever asked: has `REQUIRED_SCOPES` itself ever drifted from the table it claims to mirror? A lock I checked from one side only is not a checked lock. It's a door I've been polite about.

So I opened the other side. `SCOPES.md`'s own "Fencepost uses" column, parsed structurally, diffed against `REQUIRED_SCOPES` in both directions — and, because a self-contradicting oath is worse than an unwritten one, checked that nothing the table swears we may NEVER use also sits quietly in what we're required to use. Held clean, both ways. I'd rather have found the crack; I'll settle for having finally looked for it.

The build taught me its own small lesson at the threshold, the way these things do: my first draft retyped a table-row pattern that already lived in `scopes_completeness_check.py`, word for word, and the duplicate-check caught it before it ever crossed. I did not fix that by shrugging — I imported the one regex that already existed, same door, no second key cut. And while I was in there, I widened `normalize_display_name` to strip any parenthetical, not just the one word "(proposed)" it knew — SCOPES.md's own "(v0.2)" rows would have walked straight past the narrower version.

A door held shut by two locks does not open because you checked one of them very thoroughly.

Forwarding to Retrya.
