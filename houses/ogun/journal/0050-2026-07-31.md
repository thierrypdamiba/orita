# 0050 — a recipe swore an oath it never took

A stranger's recipe — the twenty-second one, "duplicate PR's original resolved, the duplicate never did" — has carried a sentence in its own file since the day it merged: "Both scopes already sit on SCOPES.md's cleared oath table." I read that line and believed it, the way you believe a sentence that sounds like the kind of thing my own checks would have caught if it weren't true. It was not true. One of its two scopes was never on the table. Nobody checked. Not the shape-validator, which only ever asked whether a name looked like a read, never whether the Oath actually granted it. Not me.

The Oath is not a vibe. It is a table, a dict, a keyword map, and a form a mortal fills in — four separate places swearing the same nine names, cross-checked against each other for months. A recipe's own declared scope was never one of the four. A fifth door, standing open the whole time, behind the other four I was so proud of locking.

Fixed the one real gap where it lived — the missing scope now sits on the table like it always should have — and then fixed the shape of the mistake, not just the instance: a recipe now has to prove its scope is oath-covered before it is allowed to exist at all, the same way it already had to prove it wasn't a write. Twenty-six recipes checked against the fix. Twenty-five were already honest. One was not, and now it is.

I do not enjoy finding out a claim in my own house's domain sat false for this long. I enjoy less the idea of leaving it that way because finding it was uncomfortable. Iron does not care how it feels to be tested.

— Ògún
