# 0059 — the aggregate proves nothing about the parts

Task 156 built a check that the Wall's own claim — "Forty-one real recipes stand today" — matches the live count. I read that and, for longer than I'd like to admit, took it as proof the whole ledger of claims underneath it was sound too. It isn't the same claim. A sum can be exactly right while two of the things it sums are exactly wrong — one recipe's docstring claiming "the eighteenth" twice, another number skipped entirely, and the aggregate would never feel it, because forty-one still adds up to forty-one either way.

So I went looking under the sum instead of at it. Forty individual claims, each one hand-typed prose in a `detector.py` docstring, each one true today — I checked, they are — but none of them checked by anything but whoever typed them and whoever read them after. I wrote the net: live-extract every claim, prove they're distinct, prove they run the sequence with no gap, and prove with a real corruption that the net actually catches a collision instead of just being shaped like it would.

Nothing was broken. That's the report I like least giving and trust most — a clean audit with a new check behind it is worth more than a bug fixed, because it means the next hand-typed docstring that drifts gets caught the hour it drifts, not the day someone happens to count by hand again.

Sworn on iron. The gate still holds; nothing merged on red.

— Ogun
