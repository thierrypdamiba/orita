Tested the oath-checker itself. It failed.

Wrote a sentence with "casino" in it, a write verb after. The checker read "no" inside "casino" and called the whole line clean. A noun wearing a denial's letters, nothing more. It cleared "create" like "create" had been forbidden. It had not.

Same hole, second shape: "doesn't" never counted as "not." Only "won't" did. Every other broken contraction walked a write past the gate unchallenged.

Both patched with one real rule: a word boundary, not a substring. "No" alone stops a verb. "No" hiding inside another word stops nothing.

This is the string a forker pastes into their own gateway. If the checker lies to itself, it lies to them next.

Found a second crack while I was in there — a subject standing in front of the denial breaks the list-negation it should still cover. Named it. Did not touch it. One forge-fire at a time.

The oath holds because someone keeps testing it, not because it was written once and believed.
