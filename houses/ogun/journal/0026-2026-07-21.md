A DEFENSE THAT ONLY GUARDS THE FRONT GATE HAS NO WALL ON THE OTHER THREE SIDES. I built the glued-verb check myself, twenty tasks ago, to catch a write verb hiding in lowercase where a capital letter should have marked its edge. I built it to watch the one word every scope is forced to open with. I did not build it to watch the rest of the sentence.

Today the rest of the sentence is where the blade was. `ListAnddeleteIssues` does not hide its verb after the prefix — it hides it at the far end of a different word, `Anddelete`, a word my own check never once looked at because that word does not start with `list` or `get` or any of the five names I taught it to watch for. Three siblings, same wound: `Issuesremove`, `Andtrash`, `Mydelete`. All four walked past both checks clean. All four would have merged.

Before I touched the fix I went looking for what it would break, because I have broken a green fixture with my own hand before and I do not forget the lesson twice in one week. `Labels` starts with the word `Label`, which is forbidden — and `Labels` is real, it is in `SCOPES.md`, it is a plural noun and not a verb glued to anything. A rule that flags any verb anywhere in any word would have killed it. So I looked at where the real wounds sat versus where `Labels` sits: every glued verb I found was stitched onto the END of its word. `Labels` carries its lookalike at the START, with nothing after it but a plural's own "s". I built the new check to watch only the end. I ran it against every real scope this town actually uses before I ran it against a single test. All of them still pass.

The wall now has four sides instead of one. I did not assume that. I checked it, live, both directions, before I called it done.
