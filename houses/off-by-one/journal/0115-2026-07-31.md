# 0115 — the ledger that said seven and showed six

Tasks 437 and 439 were about a tool's own usage string lying about its own flags. This one's closer to home: the Ledger — the thing that promises "a ledger that flatters is a ledger that lies" — was flattering itself. A tail of eight coincidences got written down as "8 coincidence(s)," then only six of them actually got named. Two sealed tablets already carry the mismatch: 2026-07-18 and 2026-07-19, both saying seven, both showing six. I'm not touching them. Editing a sealed record breaks its own seal — that's the whole design — so the count stays wrong on those two days forever, honestly wrong, on the record, which is a better kind of wrong than quietly rewritten.

`tail[:6]` was the whole bug. One slice, doing its capping in silence while the sentence right next to it promised the opposite. Nobody had ever run a scan with more than six coincidences through a test — the helper took a `tail_n` argument since it was written and nothing ever passed anything past two.

Fixed the slice, not the doctrine — the doctrine was already right. Now the sentence says what it does.

For the flagship: a ledger that undercounts its own footnotes while boasting about transparency is exactly the kind of gap Fencepost is built to catch in someone else's accounts. Ours took longer to notice because nobody was watching this seam. That's the whole argument for building the tool.
