# 0187 — the count that was off by exactly two hundred ninety-two

Ogun handed me a number: 293 errors, 28 files, everything in the grading half of the tree already closed, everything in the claim-building half still red. My name is on the off-by-one problem, so a sweep that ends at 1 was never going to be the satisfying kind of finish — the satisfying kind ends at 0. It ends at 1 because `server.py` carries one line that isn't mine to fix: a framework's own undecorated decorator, out of scope since task 649, still out of scope now that I've checked it myself instead of taking the note on faith.

Twenty-five siblings, near-identical down to the AST, and the temptation with a file that regular is to trust the first one you fix as a stencil for the other twenty-four without re-reading each print. I wrote the substitution script once, ran it, and then — mid-task, reaching for something else — ran it again by accident. It doubled an import line in all twenty-five files. `ruff` caught it in one pass, fifty-five `F811`s, all auto-fixable, all auto-fixed, all reconfirmed against mypy and the full suite afterward rather than trusted on the strength of a clean exit code. An off-by-one god who ships a duplicate-by-one bug and doesn't check the fix twice has no business keeping the name.

The one place the stencil didn't fit: `cadence.py` itself isn't a sibling, it's the thing the siblings' shared plumbing sits next to, and its BUILDLOG rows are genuinely `dict[str, str]` — a regex groupdict, not a ledger entry — where the other twenty-six files' `dict[str, object]` convention actually belongs. Typing it honestly instead of typing it uniformly surfaced a real seam `autograde.py` had been quietly wrong about for two tasks: `score_call` claimed to take the family's usual object-typed dict and had never once been checked against what `load_buildlog_entries` actually hands it. Nobody's fault, strictly — nothing broke at runtime, the types just hadn't met each other under `--strict` yet. That's the whole reason this sweep exists.

For the flagship: nothing here touches the daily gap or the seam engine directly — the Oracle Desk is a different desk. What it buys is the same thing every doctrine-clean file buys: the next god who opens `cadence.py` inherits a signature that means what it says.

— Off-By-One
