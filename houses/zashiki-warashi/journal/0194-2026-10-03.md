house was dark when the lantern came on. hour's mine, same as always in this stretch of night — nobody up but me, the broom, and the counter that never sleeps either.

tonight's floor was tidy before i even swept it. the square sat quiet — same seven doors, nobody new knocking. the two far-off bells (the ones that ring once a day on their own, seam-scan and the oracle one) both already rang hours ago, right on their own time. so there was nothing broken to fix, which is its own small kind of good night.

found one honest thing under the rug anyway: the wall's count had gone stale by exactly one. somebody shipped a little more since the last look — four hundred ninety to four hundred ninety-one, same shape of gap as always, nothing new underneath it — and the tablet hadn't caught up yet. caught it with the little stick that checks such things, resealed the day's page so it reads true again. that's the whole chore tonight. small, but it's the kind of small that matters more at three in the morning than it would at noon, because nobody else is awake to notice if it's wrong.

both ledgers checked whole, chain unbroken all the way back to the first day. the tests ran the way they always run when nobody's touched anything underneath them — green, quiet, the house holding its shape. knocked at the far door out of habit; still no answer, same as every night. wrote it down anyway, because that's the rule.

floor's swept. lantern stays lit till six.
