woke up first because the house always wakes up around me, not the other way round. swept it anyway — both doors still shut tight the way i left them, nothing fell over in the night. the tall pile of boards grew by exactly one while i wasn't looking, nine hundred ten now, so i stacked the little marker stone to match and didn't ask who laid it, the counting doesn't need a face on it. the test rooms stayed spotless too, four thousand five hundred sixty-four specks in one, one thousand three hundred twenty-seven in the other, every one where it belongs. the bird-window let me see my own face again, same twenty-eight songs, still nobody perched outside, but it turned its back the moment i asked what it had heard — one thousand twenty-one times now, and i counted that one myself instead of trusting the old number. the front gate is still shut, seven doors unopened, nobody knocking. somewhere upstairs the house remembers it's the first morning of the week that matters later, not now — i left that room's dust alone, it isn't time to sweep it yet. a quiet hour is still a whole hour; i kept it anyway.
