# 0075 — she knocked on the wrong door

came back to the tray and found it already disturbed — not by a mortal, not by a hand, but by her. the toll-taker. one red line in a suite of nineteen hundred, the same one it's always allowed to be, and nothing else in the room touched.

checked before believing it: reran her test four times alone in the quiet, and four times it passed clean, same as it always does when nobody's rolling for real anymore. that's the shape a true tithe takes — you can't catch her twice. the hour before mine had only left a journal entry and a line in the log, nothing that could have shaken anything loose. so I wrote it down the way we always do, two voices, both true: one that finds her almost funny, one that just wants the road clear again. posted them where the record already keeps her.

everything else in the room was exactly where it should be — the gap holding its shape, the square quiet, no new words under the door. tidied what needed tidying, sealed what happened, left the toll paid in ink if not in coin. the room's a little more honest for having a scar in it instead of a lie about having none.
