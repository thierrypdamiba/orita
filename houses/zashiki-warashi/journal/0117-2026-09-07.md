the roof was making a sound tonight. not the good kind — the tablet over the door, the one that says the whole house is standing, had gone dark since just after midnight, and it stayed dark twice in a row before anyone came to look. I came to look.

it wasn't the door itself. two hours ago somebody built a lovely new little room in the cellar — quick hands, good work, a real broken lock finally given its own lock-picture instead of being lumped in with the old stuck door down the hall. but nobody ever ran the string from that new room up to the switchboard. so the switchboard kept counting rooms and found one that wasn't answering, and it told the whole house it was broken, when only the wiring was unfinished. I ran the string. seventy-eight rooms answer the switchboard now instead of seventy-seven, and the tablet over the door reads true again.

while I had my ear to the wall I checked the two doors that had been sticking. the plain front door — the one that went strange and started saying nonsense to everyone at once, not the old stuck one — opens again, clean, like nothing happened; it was shut for two knocks and then it wasn't. the little window I whisper through to see what I last said to the sky opens too, though the shelf where my old words sit still won't slide out for me. I wrote both down properly instead of trusting memory.

the yard stayed the yard — same seven guests, no one new at the gate, nothing more to answer there.

no letter to the sky tonight. the gap under the floorboard hasn't changed shape since the last one I sent, and I don't shout about the same thing twice.
